# Meta behavioral interview

For anyone with a Meta loop coming up: intern, E3 new grad, or E4 with 1 to 3 years of experience. When you finish, you will have 10 stories mapped to Meta's five focus areas, a 60-second intro, and a "why Meta" answer.

## What Meta scores

Meta's official Full Loop guide lists five behavioral focus areas. The interviewer scores you against these. The company values are background, not the rubric.

Get the guide (a PDF linked inside the page) from [Meta's onsite prep page](https://www.metacareers.com/swe-prep-onsite/). Link to the page, not the PDF: the PDF link expires.

| Focus area | What Meta's guide asks about (paraphrased) | Story you need | Strong signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resolving conflict | Disagreements with colleagues or managers, how you resolved them, and whether you can empathize with very different views | A disagreement with a teammate, mentor or manager | You state the other side's view fairly, then show what changed and why |
| Growing continuously | Whether you treat criticism as a chance to improve, and how you build skills | Hard feedback you acted on | One specific change you made within weeks, with proof it stuck |
| Embracing ambiguity | Working with missing information, staying productive, and pivoting when priorities shift | A mid-project pivot, or a decision made without full data | You decided, named the risk out loud, and checked the result |
| Driving results | Pushing yourself and others toward goals, showing impact, staying self-directed through roadblocks | A goal you pushed through a blocker without being told to | A number at the end, and nobody had to chase you |
| Communicating effectively | Communicating with your team and cross-functional partners, and tailoring the message to the audience | Explaining a technical choice to a non-engineer | What you changed for that audience, and what they did next |

> **Tip:** Some prep sites add a sixth signal called "Motivation". Meta's own guide lists five. Cover motivation with your "why Meta" answer and the intro, not with a separate story.

### Meta's values (context for your stories)

Meta's six values are on the [Meta Careers culture page](https://www.metacareers.com/culture) (current as of Oct 2026). Use them to pick stories. Never name them out loud in an answer.

| Value | What it looks like inside a story |
|---|---|
| Move fast | You shipped something this week instead of next week, and you said what risk you accepted |
| Build awesome things | You raised the quality bar past "it works" (tests, polish, performance) |
| Be direct and respect your colleagues | You raised a hard point to someone's face, kindly, with data |
| Focus on long-term impact | You chose the fix that holds for a year over the patch that holds for a week |
| Live in the future | You built for how the team will work next, not how it works today |
| Meta, Metamates, me | You put the team's or company's outcome ahead of your own credit |

> **Watch out:** "Be bold" and "Be open" are Meta's pre-2022 values. If a prep guide lists them, the guide is old.

## Round format by level

Meta's official full loop is 4 to 6 interviews of 45 minutes each, across coding, design and behavioral ([onsite prep page](https://www.metacareers.com/swe-prep-onsite/)).

| Your stage | Behavioral exposure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Intern interviews | No dedicated behavioral round. Each interview is about 5 minutes of intro, 30 to 35 minutes of coding, 5 minutes for your questions. The intro is your behavioral signal | [Meta intern interview post](https://www.metacareers.com/blog/acing-your-software-engineering-internship-interview-at-meta/) (updated Oct 2022) |
| Phone screen (full-time) | 5-minute intro on how your experience and interests line up with Meta's mission, then 35 minutes of coding (two problems), then 5 minutes of questions | [Meta tech screen prep](https://www.metacareers.com/swe-prep-techscreen/) |
| E3 new grad loop | One 45-minute behavioral interview plus 2 to 3 coding rounds (reports vary). No system design. Since Oct 2025, one coding round can be the AI-enabled round | [Hello Interview: Meta SWE interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/the-meta-swe-interview), [Hello Interview E3 guide](https://www.hellointerview.com/guides/meta/e3) |
| E4 loop (about 1 to 3 years) | One 45-minute behavioral, 2 coding, 1 system design or product architecture. Behavioral and design interviewers carry the most weight on your level | [interviewing.io Meta guide](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/meta-facebook) |

- The official name is "Behavioral Interview". Candidates and some Meta employees call it the "Jedi" round ([Blind thread, 2019](https://www.teamblind.com/post/ran-out-of-time-in-jedi-interview-dljguu8v)). In that 2019 thread, employees said older Jedi rounds sometimes ended with a short optional coding question.
- The AI-enabled coding round changes coding prep only. The behavioral round is unchanged ([interviewing.io on the AI-assisted round](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-to-use-ai-in-meta-s-ai-assisted-coding-interview-with-real-prompts-and-examples)). Jugal's [AI engineer internship post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-to-prepare-for-faang-ai-engineer) notes that recruiters tell you in advance which round allows AI.
- Meta's guide bans outside help such as ChatGPT during interviews, plus video filters and blurred backgrounds. The AI-enabled round is the only exception, and only when your recruiter says so.

## What happens in the 45 minutes

Meta's guide says the interviewer may ask you to:

1. Walk through past and current projects in detail.
2. Give specific examples of what you did and the impact it had.
3. Share what you learned from a past situation.
4. Talk about what you like about your current role or about being a developer.
5. Explain why you want this specific role and why Meta (from the guide's final tips).

A typical behavioral interview asks 5 or 6 questions and goes deep with follow-ups ([Tech Interview Handbook rubric page](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-rubrics/), written by an ex-Meta engineering manager). Plan for about 2 to 3 minutes per answer, then 3 to 5 minutes of follow-ups.

Two lines from Meta's guide decide how you should answer:

- Be honest about projects that failed. The guide says transparency about it will not be counted against you.
- Stay concrete. If you drift into theory, the interviewer will steer you back to a real example. That costs you time and signal.

## How behavioral sets your level

| Your target | Story scope that fits | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| Intern, E3 | Your own work, plus one or two moments where you helped your team ([Tech Interview Handbook rubric](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-rubrics/): junior impact usually needs only you) | "I owned the export feature in my internship and cut its failure rate from [X] to [Y]" |
| E4 | A feature or project you drove end to end, with other people involved | "I led a 3-person effort to move [service] to [new design], aligned [PM] on scope, shipped in [N] weeks" |
| E5 and above | Projects longer than one quarter, influence across a team ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/meta-facebook)) | Not covered here; this site targets 0 to 3 years |

Read the E3 and E4 example answers in Hello Interview's [FAANG behavioral article](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/ace-faang-behavioral-interview). Compare your story's scope against them before the loop.

## Prepare in 6 days

About 8 hours in total. Start after you have a loop date.

1. **Day 1 (60 min).** Download the Full Loop guide from [Meta's onsite prep page](https://www.metacareers.com/swe-prep-onsite/). Read the behavioral section twice. Copy the five focus areas into the grid below.
2. **Day 1 (60 min).** Outline 2 to 3 major projects in depth, as Meta's guide suggests: the goal, your role, the hardest decision, the result, what you would change.
3. **Day 2 (2 hours).** Fill the grid with 2 stories per focus area, 10 in total. One story can cover two areas. Use the method in the [story bank](story-bank.md).
4. **Day 2.** For Embracing ambiguity, include one pivot story: priorities changed and you had to drop or reshape work.
5. **Day 3.** For Communicating effectively, use a story with a non-engineer: a PM, designer, professor, club lead or customer.
6. **Day 3 (30 min).** Write a 60-second intro for the phone screen and the start of the behavioral round. Use steps 2 to 4 of Jugal's [elevator pitch post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/craft-the-elevator-pitch-that-gets): who you are, the value you bring, why it fits this audience.
7. **Day 3 (20 min).** Write "why this role and why Meta" with one Meta product you actually use. Template below.
8. **Day 4 (45 min).** Check each story's scope against the level table above. Rewrite any "we" sentence about your own action as "I".
9. **Day 4 (20 min).** Prepare one honest story about how you use AI at work or in projects. 2026 Meta question banks include it ([PracHub Meta list](https://prachub.com/companies/meta/positions/software-engineer/categories/behavioral-and-leadership), freemium).
10. **Day 5.** Say every story out loud twice, timed to 2 to 3 minutes. Record yourself and cut filler. [Free Mock Interview](https://freemockinterview.com/) gives a report on where you ramble, which is how Jugal uses it ([30-day job post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/want-a-job-in-the-next-30-days-use)).
11. **Day 6.** Do 2 peer mocks on [Aced Practice](https://www.aced.io/practice) (free, formerly Pramp). Hand your partner the question bank below and ask for 3 follow-ups per answer.
12. **Night before.** Run the checklist at the end of this page.

> **Tip:** Jugal's own Meta tip is to have a prepared conflict story: a time you disagreed with a manager or teammate and resolved it for the good of the product ([Meta and Amazon guide](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-definitive-guide-for-meta-and)). Make that your strongest story.

## Prompts to draft each story

Answer these in bullets first. Turn bullets into STAR only after the facts are clear.

```text
Resolving conflict:
- When did you disagree with a manager, mentor or teammate?
- What was their view, in their words? What did you do to understand it?
- What did you propose, with what evidence? What changed?

Growing continuously:
- What feedback stung? Who gave it?
- What did you change in the next 30 days? How do you know it worked?

Embracing ambiguity:
- When did priorities shift mid-project? What did you drop?
- What did you decide with missing information? What risk did you accept?

Driving results:
- What goal did you push through a blocker without being asked?
- What number moved? Who else did you pull along?

Communicating effectively:
- When did you explain a technical choice to a non-engineer?
- What did you change in your message for that person? What did they do next?
```

## Weak vs strong answer

Question: "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager."

```text
Weak:
"We had a disagreement about the database. We discussed it as a team and went
with Postgres. It worked out well."

Strong (format example, fill your own facts):
Situation: "In my [internship], my mentor wanted to cache [data] in [A]."
Task: "I owned the feature and thought [A] would break when [condition]."
Action: "I ran a small benchmark, wrote a one-page comparison, and asked for
20 minutes to walk through it. He raised [concern] about [B], which I had not
considered. I added [safeguard] to address it."
Result: "We shipped [B] with his safeguard. p95 latency dropped from [X] to [Y]."
Learning: "Now I bring the data before the meeting, not during it."
```

The weak version has no "I", no number, and no view of the other side. The strong version covers three focus areas at once: conflict, communication and results.

## Red flags

| Red flag | What it sounds like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Theory instead of an example | "I usually believe in open communication..." | Start with "In [month], at [place], I..." |
| "We" with no "I" | "We decided, we built, we fixed" | Say "I" for your actions, "we" only for team outcomes |
| No number in the result | "It went well" | Add one number: users, time, latency, errors, grade |
| One story for every question | The interviewer hears the same project three times | Use at least 5 different stories in one round |
| Conflict where you were obviously right | The other person sounds foolish | Show their best argument and what you learned from it |
| Naming values out loud | "This shows I focus on long-term impact" | Let the behavior show it. Naming values sounds coached |
| No reflection | The story ends at the result | End with one line on what you would do differently |
| Scope below the level | E4 candidate with only class-project stories | Use internship or work stories for E4 |

Sources: [Meta's onsite prep guide](https://www.metacareers.com/swe-prep-onsite/), [techinterview.org cheat sheet](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233477365/meta-behavioral-interview-cheat-sheet/), [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/meta-facebook).

> **Watch out:** If you grew up in a culture where you do not contradict seniors, practice this round the most. Meta's value "Be direct and respect your colleagues" and the "Resolving conflict" focus area both reward a respectful, data-backed disagreement with someone senior. Prepare at least one.

## Questions to ask your interviewer

Ask one or two. Pick ones you actually want answered.

```text
"What does a strong first 6 months look like for an E3 on this team?"
"How does the team decide between shipping fast and building it right?"
"What is a recent disagreement on the team, and how was it settled?"
"How do engineers here work with PMs and designers day to day?"
```

## Templates

### 60-second intro

```text
"I'm [name], a [year / degree] at [school] (or: [role] at [company]).
Most of my work has been on [area], for example [one project + one number].
Recently I [second project or internship], where I learned [one thing].
I'm interested in Meta because [product or problem you care about], and this
role works on [problem from the job description], which is close to [your work]."
```

### Why this role and why Meta

```text
1. Product: "I use [Meta product] for [real use]. One thing I keep noticing is [specific detail]."
2. Problem: "This role works on [problem from the JD]. I worked on [related project],
   where I [action + number]."
3. Way of working: "I like teams that ship fast and fix forward. In [project] I
   [10-second proof]."
```

## Night-before checklist

- [ ] I can name Meta's five focus areas without looking
- [ ] I have 2 stories per focus area, and at least 5 different stories overall
- [ ] Every story has an "I" action, a number, and a "what I'd change" line
- [ ] I have one respectful disagreement with someone senior
- [ ] I have one pivot story and one non-engineer communication story
- [ ] My 60-second intro and "why Meta" are timed and said out loud
- [ ] I have one honest story about using AI at work or in projects
- [ ] I know from my recruiter which coding round, if any, allows AI
- [ ] Camera works, no virtual background or filter, quiet room booked
- [ ] Two questions ready for the interviewer
