# Netflix behavioral interview

For anyone interviewing at Netflix, where culture rounds can sink a strong technical loop. You finish having read the culture memo, with 8 stories (one per value), 2 feedback stories and an honest view on the keeper test.

## Start with the culture memo

Netflix expects candidates to have read its culture memo. Interviewers told interviewing.io that candidates who skip it fail the behavioral round ([interviewing.io Netflix guide](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/netflix)). The memo lives on [jobs.netflix.com/culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture). The [PDF version](https://jobs.netflix.com/netflix-culture.pdf) is 5 pages, footer "Last updated: June 2024".

1. Download [the memo PDF](https://jobs.netflix.com/netflix-culture.pdf) and read it end to end once (5 pages, about 30 minutes).
2. Read it again with a pen. Mark every phrase that describes a behavior, such as "farming for dissent".
3. Copy the 8 values into the story grid below.
4. Write one sentence per value: "The closest I have come to this is [moment]."
5. Note anything in the memo you disagree with or feel unsure about. Interviewers may ask.

### The four principles

| Principle | What it means in plain words |
|---|---|
| The Dream Team | Netflix wants a team of high performers, and says it models itself on a professional sports team, not a family |
| People Over Process | People get context and freedom to decide, instead of rules and approvals |
| Uncomfortably Exciting | Bold bets and calculated risks are expected |
| Great and Always Better | Improve constantly. Good enough today is not good enough tomorrow |

### The 8 values, turned into stories

The value names are from the memo. The meanings are short paraphrases. Read the full definitions on [the memo page](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture).

| Value | What the memo means | Story prompt to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Selflessness | You seek what is best for Netflix, not for yourself or your team | A time you helped another person or team at a cost to your own goal |
| Judgment | You choose long-term solutions over short-term fixes, decide well despite ambiguity, and use data to inform intuition | A time you chose the lasting fix over the quick patch |
| Candor | You give and receive feedback willingly, and admit mistakes openly | A time you gave hard feedback, and a time you admitted a mistake publicly |
| Creativity | You welcome new ideas | A new idea you proposed, or someone else's idea you adopted |
| Courage | You are willing to risk failure or challenge the status quo | A time you questioned how something had always been done |
| Inclusion | You recognize your biases and work to counteract them | A time you changed a decision after noticing your own bias |
| Curiosity | You learn rapidly and eagerly | A topic you learned fast because a problem needed it |
| Resilience | You adapt quickly when circumstances change | A plan that broke, and how you adjusted |

### Phrases you should be able to explain

| Phrase in the memo | Plain meaning | Story it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Keeper test | Managers ask "if X wanted to leave, would I fight to keep them?" or "knowing everything I know today, would I hire X again?" If not, Netflix parts ways | Your honest view of working under it (see below) |
| Context not control | Leaders share context and let people decide, instead of approving every step | A time you made a good call because someone gave you context |
| Informed captain | One person owns each decision after gathering input | A decision you owned end to end |
| Farming for dissent | Actively ask people to disagree before deciding | A time you asked for pushback before deciding |
| Disagree then commit | Argue your view, then fully support the decision once made | A time you lost an argument and still helped it succeed |
| Highly aligned, loosely coupled | Teams agree on goals, then work independently | Working with another team without micromanaging it |
| Extraordinary candor | Feedback is direct and frequent, up and down | Feedback you gave to someone more senior |
| Unusually responsible people | Freedom works because people act responsibly | A time you made a judgment call with no rule to follow |

> **Watch out:** Many prep sites still call Netflix culture "Freedom and Responsibility". The current memo does not use that phrase (checked Oct 2026). Use the current term, "People Over Process".

## Round format

Netflix publishes the shape of the student process. Most detailed public reports describe experienced-hire loops, so ask your recruiter which of the rounds below you will have.

| Stage | What happens | Source |
|---|---|---|
| New grad (official) | A take-home assessment, then two interview rounds covering technical, role-specific and behavioral skills. Roles usually post in late September or October | [Netflix new grads page](https://jobs.netflix.com/careers/new-grads) |
| Intern (official) | A take-home assessment, then about 2 to 3 interview rounds with the same three areas. Roles post from mid-August to early September | [Netflix internships page](https://jobs.netflix.com/careers/internships) |
| Recruiter culture screen (about 30 min) | Motivation, how you read the memo, logistics such as visa sponsorship, location and compensation | [PracHub report](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/answer-netflix-culture-screen-questions) (freemium) |
| Experienced loop (about 8 interviews) | Mostly system design, then behavioral, then coding. Team-dependent, and you can interview with more than one team at once | [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/netflix) |
| Behavioral interviews | Usually run by an HR business partner (HRBP) and 1 to 2 directors (when there are two, one is from a partner org). Hiring managers do not lead them | interviewing.io |
| "Dream Team" interview | A more intense behavioral round run by a director: scale, accountability, candor, risk-taking | interviewing.io |

- interviewing.io states that you will be rejected if you fail the behavioral screen. A strong coding round does not offset it.
- Jugal's Netflix breakdown says the loop weighs culture fit and system design heavily, even for IC roles. His summary: Netflix equals ownership, design and culture alignment ([How to crack FAANG interviews, Part 5](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-to-crack-faang-interviews-part-020)).
- Search open roles on [Netflix jobs](https://explore.jobs.netflix.net/careers) and read the team's job description before the culture screen.

## Prepare in 7 days

About 9 hours in total.

1. **Day 1 (60 min).** Read [the culture memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture) twice using the 5 steps at the top of this page.
2. **Day 2 (2 hours).** Write 8 stories, one per value, in STAR format. Each story shows a behavior. None of them names the value. Use the [story bank](story-bank.md) method for finding stories.
3. **Day 3 (60 min).** Write 2 feedback stories with the template below: one where you gave hard feedback (upward if you can), one where you received it and changed.
4. **Day 3 (30 min).** Write 1 informed captain story: you owned a decision, asked for dissent, then the team committed.
5. **Day 4 (30 min).** Prepare the recruiter screen: why Netflix, why this team, your visa status, location preference and a compensation approach. See [negotiation scripts](../negotiation/scripts.md) for deflecting the number.
6. **Day 4 (20 min).** Decide your honest view on the keeper test and on Netflix's content slate. The memo raises both, and interviewing.io's recorded [Netflix behavioral mock](https://interviewing.io/mocks/netflix-behavioral-interview-1) includes a keeper test question. Watch it (free) and note how the interviewer probes.
7. **Day 5 (45 min).** Practice the question bank out loud. Answer both halves of every feedback question.
8. **Day 6 (30 min).** Prepare 2 questions for each director (list below).
9. **Day 7.** Do 2 mocks on [Aced Practice](https://www.aced.io/practice) (formerly Pramp; free monthly credits) or one paid mock on [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/mocks). Ask your partner to challenge your weakest value story.

## Feedback stories

4 of the 10 reported questions below are about feedback. Prepare both directions.

```text
Giving feedback
[ ] Who: peer / someone senior / cross-functional (PM, data scientist, designer)
[ ] What you saw: the specific behavior, not a personality judgment
[ ] Exact words you used (Situation, Behavior, Impact):
    "In [situation], when you [behavior], it caused [impact]. Could we try [ask]?"
[ ] Why you said it to them directly, instead of to someone else
[ ] What changed afterward (a number or a visible behavior)

Receiving feedback
[ ] Who gave it, and what they said
[ ] Your first reaction, honestly
[ ] What you changed in the next 30 days
[ ] Proof it stuck (a later example)
```

> **Tip:** The memo says candor is especially hard when you give feedback to someone more senior or from a different background, or if you come from a culture or company where deference is the norm. Many Indian and international students come from exactly that setting. Prepare one story where you respectfully gave feedback upward, so the concern is answered before anyone raises it.

## Informed captain story

```text
Decision: [what had to be decided, by when]
Why I owned it: [role or volunteered]
Dissent I asked for: "I asked [who] to argue against [option]. They said [concern]."
What I decided and why: [choice + the data that settled it]
How the team committed: [what people who disagreed did next]
Result: [number]
What I'd do differently: [one line]
```

## The keeper test: how to talk about it

You may be asked how you feel about it. There is no single right answer, but there are wrong ones.

| Answer type | Example | How it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Fear | "That sounds stressful, I'd worry about being fired" | Signals you expect to be a low performer |
| Fake enthusiasm | "I love it, it's perfect" | Sounds rehearsed, and gets probed |
| Honest and specific | "I like knowing where I stand. I'd want feedback early and often so a keeper decision is never a surprise. In [project] I asked my lead for weekly feedback and it helped me fix [X] fast." | Shows candor, judgment and self-awareness |

The memo also says that if you would find it hard to support the breadth of Netflix's content, Netflix is probably not the place for you. Decide honestly before the loop.

## Question bank

Reported questions from candidates, then practice prompts built from the memo.

| # | Question | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell me about a time you had to give constructive feedback to a teammate or cross-functional partner when something wasn't going well. | Candor | [PracHub Netflix list](https://prachub.com/companies/netflix/positions/software-engineer/categories/behavioral-and-leadership) |
| 2 | Tell me about a time you received critical feedback. How did you respond? What did you change afterward? | Candor, Curiosity | [PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/how-do-you-give-and-receive-feedback) |
| 3 | Describe a time you strengthened team culture or upheld values under pressure. | Selflessness, Courage | PracHub |
| 4 | How do you handle disagreements and mentor across functions? | Candor, Inclusion | PracHub |
| 5 | Walk through a complex project you owned end to end: goals, constraints, trade-offs, risks, metrics, failures, what you'd do differently. | Judgment | PracHub (paraphrased) |
| 6 | Which parts of the memo map to your past roles, and where have you shown them? | All | PracHub (recruiter screen) |
| 7 | What is your greatest weakness? | Candor | PracHub |
| 8 | What is your favorite, latest or most significant project? | Judgment, Creativity | [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/netflix) (theme) |
| 9 | Why Netflix, and what do you want in your next role? | Motivation | interviewing.io (theme) |
| 10 | Give three real examples of collaboration, feedback and constructive conflict. | Candor, Selflessness | PracHub |

Practice prompts from the memo (not reported questions, but they target the same behaviors):

```text
1. Tell me about a time you were the informed captain on a decision.
2. Tell me about a time you farmed for dissent before deciding.
3. Tell me about a time you disagreed, then committed.
4. Tell me about a mistake you admitted openly. Who did you tell, and when?
5. Tell me about a time you chose a long-term fix over a short-term patch.
6. Tell me about a time you challenged the status quo.
7. Tell me about a time you helped someone else succeed at a cost to yourself.
```

## Red flags

| Red flag | Why it hurts at Netflix | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Brilliant-jerk signals | The memo says there is no place on the Dream Team for people who don't treat colleagues with decency and respect | Show you can be direct and kind in the same story |
| Vague feedback stories | "I gave them some feedback and it went fine" | Quote what you said, and show what changed |
| Not having read the memo | interviewing.io says Netflix expects you to | Read it twice and use its current terms |
| Hiding your opinion out of deference | The memo calls out deference cultures directly | Prepare one respectful upward-feedback story |
| Calling Netflix a family | The memo says sports team, not family | Talk about high performance and trust |
| Discomfort with the content slate | The memo says Netflix may not be for you | Decide honestly before you apply |

## Questions to ask directors

```text
"Can you share a recent decision where an informed captain went against the majority?"
"How does this team farm for dissent before big decisions?"
"What context does a new engineer get, and what do they decide alone?"
"What does great look like for someone at my level after a year here?"
```

## Night-before checklist

- [ ] I read the current memo twice and can name the 4 principles and 8 values
- [ ] 8 stories, one per value, none of them naming the value
- [ ] 2 feedback stories: one given (ideally upward), one received
- [ ] 1 informed captain story with real dissent in it
- [ ] An honest, specific view on the keeper test
- [ ] Recruiter screen logistics ready: visa, location, compensation approach
- [ ] 2 questions per director
- [ ] Every story has a number and a "what I'd do differently" line
