# Microsoft behavioral interview

For interns, new grads (level 59 or 60) and early-career engineers (61 or 62) interviewing at Microsoft. When you finish, you will have STAR(R) stories for growth mindset and Microsoft's values, a strengths and weaknesses answer, and a plan for the behavioral minutes at the start of each round.

## What Microsoft scores

Microsoft publishes two lists. Prepare stories for both.

| Official list | Items | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Values | Respect, Integrity, Accountability | [Microsoft About page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/about) |
| Culture | Growth mindset, Customer obsessed, Diverse and inclusive, One Microsoft | [Microsoft Careers culture page](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/culture) |

Microsoft's interview tips describe growth mindset as being "true learners who can acquire new skills rapidly" ([interview tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/interview-tips.html)). This is the trait most stories should show.

| Trait | What it looks like in an answer | Story to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Growth mindset | You learned something new fast, or changed after feedback or failure | Learned a new stack in [N] days and shipped with it |
| Customer obsessed | You started from what the user needed, not what was easy to build | Changed a feature after watching [N] users struggle |
| Diverse and inclusive | You made room for a quieter or different voice and the result improved | Brought a teammate's idea into a design review |
| One Microsoft | You helped another team or person succeed, beyond your own task | Unblocked another team's integration |
| Respect | You disagreed without dismissing the other person | Disagreement with a peer where you adopted part of their idea |
| Integrity | You told the truth when it cost you something | Reported your own bug before anyone noticed |
| Accountability | You owned a miss and fixed it, with no blame | A missed deadline you recovered from |

Prep guides add a competency list: Collaboration, Drive for Results, Customer Focus, Influencing for Impact, Judgment, Adaptability ([Aced, formerly Exponent](https://www.aced.io/blog/microsoft-interview-process)). It is not on an official Microsoft page. Use it as extra rows in your grid, not as the main frame.

> **Watch out:** Some prep sites cite "Create clarity, Generate energy, Deliver success" as what Microsoft looks for. We did not find these on Microsoft's careers or interview pages (Oct 2026). Do not build your prep around them.

## STAR(R): Microsoft's own answer format

Microsoft's [interview tips page](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/interview-tips.html) recommends STAR(R): Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflection. The extra R is what you learned or would do differently. Three more official tips matter here:

- Ask clarifying questions and state your assumptions before you answer a scenario or write code.
- Prepare questions to ask the interviewer.
- Have a vision beyond the role you applied for. Microsoft says you can build a career across many teams.

```text
Situation (1 to 2 sentences): [Where, when, what was at stake.]
Task (1 sentence): [What I personally owned.]
Action (3 to 4 sentences, "I" not "we"): [Step 1. Step 2. The hard choice and why.]
Result (1 to 2 sentences, with a number): [What moved, who benefited.]
Reflection (1 sentence): [What I learned, or what I would do differently next time.]
```

## Round format

Microsoft usually has no single behavioral round. Behavioral questions are mixed into most interviews, often in the first 5 to 15 minutes before coding or design ([Aced](https://www.aced.io/blog/microsoft-interview-process), candidate reports). Your stories have to be short.

| Stage | Behavioral content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Official structure | Most interviews include 2 to 4 conversations with potential teammates and cross-functional colleagues, each up to an hour | [Microsoft hiring tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips) |
| Students (interns, new grads) | An initial interview and, if selected, a final interview. All virtual. Expect behavioral and technical questions in each | [Microsoft student interviewing](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/student-interviewing) |
| Intern final round | Usually 2 technical interviews on the same day, with resume and project questions alongside coding. Some candidates get a third behavioral round (reported). Microsoft says to allow up to 90 days for a decision and that it cannot speed up review for competing offer deadlines | [University internship FAQ](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/universityinternship), [intern interviews](../internships/intern-interviews.md) |
| Full-time loop | 4 to 5 back-to-back interviews of 45 to 60 minutes, each mixing behavioral, resume and coding or design. Some 2025 to 2026 candidate reports show 3 to 4 | [Aced](https://www.aced.io/blog/microsoft-interview-process) |
| AA ("As Appropriate") round | A final interview with a senior person, usually only if earlier rounds go well. Mix of behavioral, project discussion and sometimes coding or low-level design (candidate reports, 2025) | [GfG: SDE-1 Hyderabad](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/interview-experiences/microsoft-interview-experience-for-sde-1-hyderabad/), [GfG: SDE II level 61](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/interview-experiences/microsoft-interview-experience-for-sde-ii-level-61/) |

Three more facts shape how you prepare:

- The process is mostly team-dependent, and interviewers are not given a shared rubric ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/microsoft)). Expect different styles in each round.
- interviewing.io calls behavioral the least important round at Microsoft. The screen is for friendly people who can do the work and do not blame others. A plain behavioral answer is unlikely to sink you alone. Blame or negativity can.
- AI rule: show your own skills without outside help unless the interviewer explicitly allows it. Using AI to prepare is fine ([Microsoft hiring tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips)).

> **Tip:** The AA round is not on any official Microsoft page. Do not ask "is this the AA round?". Treat every interviewer the same, and keep one fresh story in reserve for a senior interviewer in the last round.

## Time budget inside one round

This is a planning guide, not an official split. It protects your coding time.

| Part of a 45 to 60 minute round | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Intro and behavioral | 5 to 15 | 60-second intro, then 1 to 3 stories of about 2 minutes each |
| Coding or design | 30 to 40 | Clarify, state assumptions, code, test with your own cases |
| Your questions | 5 | One or two real questions |

If a behavioral answer passes 3 minutes, stop and ask: "Should I go deeper on any part?" Rambling here costs you coding time later.

## Prepare in 5 days

About 6 hours in total.

1. **Day 1 (45 min).** Read Microsoft's [interview tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/interview-tips.html) and [student interviewing page](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/student-interviewing). Note the STAR(R) model and the AI rule.
2. **Day 1 (60 min).** List 8 stories from internships, projects, TA work, hackathons and clubs. Use the [story bank](story-bank.md) method.
3. **Day 2 (90 min).** Rewrite each story in STAR(R). Add one Reflection line to every story: "Next time I would...".
4. **Day 2 (30 min).** Map one story each to Respect, Integrity and Accountability, and two stories to growth mindset (template below).
5. **Day 3 (30 min).** Write your strengths and weaknesses answer now. interviewing.io notes many candidates struggle with this question because they invent an answer on the spot.
6. **Day 3 (20 min).** Pick one Microsoft product you use and one concrete improvement. This is a common question.
7. **Day 3 (20 min).** Write "why Microsoft, why this team" (template below). Read the mission on the [About page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/about) first.
8. **Day 4.** Say every story out loud, timed to 2 minutes. Cut setup to under 30 seconds.
9. **Day 5.** Do 1 to 2 mocks on [Aced Practice](https://www.aced.io/practice) (free). Ask your partner to open with 2 behavioral questions, then give you a coding problem, like a real Microsoft round.
10. **Night before.** Run the checklist at the end of this page.

## Growth mindset: three stories to have

| Story | What it proves | Weak version | Strong version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learned fast | You pick up new skills quickly | "I'm a quick learner." | "I had never used [tool]. I read the docs for 2 evenings, built a toy version, then shipped [feature] in [N] days. It handled [number]." |
| Changed after feedback | You treat criticism as information | "I take feedback well." | "My mentor said my PRs were too large to review. For the next month I kept them under 200 lines. Review time dropped from [X] days to [Y]." |
| Failure with a lesson | You own misses and change | "I haven't really failed at anything." | "I promised a demo without testing on real data. It crashed. I told my lead the same day, fixed it in [N] hours, and now I test on production-like data first." |

## Strengths and weaknesses

```text
Strength (pick one the job needs):
"One strength is [skill]. For example, in [project] I [action] which led to [number].
Teammates usually ask me for help with [specific thing]."

Weakness (real, not a disguised strength, with progress):
"Something I'm working on is [real weakness, e.g. saying yes to too many tasks].
In [situation] it caused [small, honest consequence].
Since then I [specific habit], and in [recent example] it meant [result]."
```

> **Watch out:** "I'm a perfectionist" and "I work too hard" are not weaknesses. Interviewers hear them every day. Pick a real one that will not stop you from doing this job.

## Templates

### Favorite Microsoft product and one improvement

```text
"I use [product, e.g. VS Code / GitHub / Teams / Excel] for [real use], about [how often].
What works well is [specific feature].
One thing I'd improve is [specific friction]. I'd measure it by [metric], and I'd
start by [small experiment] before building anything big."
```

### Why Microsoft, why this team

```text
1. Product or platform: "I use [product] for [real use]. The part I keep noticing is [detail]."
2. Team problem: "This team works on [problem from the JD]. I worked on [related project],
   where I [action + number]."
3. Culture: "Microsoft talks about growth mindset. In [project] I [learned X fast / changed
   after feedback], and I want a team that works that way."
4. Beyond the role: "Longer term I'd like to grow into [area], and Microsoft has teams in [areas]."
```

Microsoft's interview tips ask for a vision beyond the role. Line 4 covers it in one sentence.

## Red flags

| Red flag | Why it hurts at Microsoft | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Blaming a teammate, manager or school | interviewing.io says the screen is for people who do not blame others | Describe what you did and what you learned |
| Negativity about past teams | Same reason | One neutral sentence about the problem, then move to your actions |
| No Reflection line | Microsoft's own format asks for it | End every story with "Next time I would..." |
| Inventing a weakness live | Sounds fake, and interviewing.io calls it out | Write it before the interview |
| Jumping into a scenario without questions | Official tip: clarify and state assumptions | Ask 1 to 2 questions first |
| 5-minute stories in a coding round | Eats your coding time | Two minutes, then offer more detail |
| Saying "nothing" when asked for questions | Official tip: prepare questions | Bring 2 real questions |

## Questions to ask your interviewer

```text
"How does the team show growth mindset in code reviews or postmortems?"
"What would someone joining at my level work on in the first quarter?"
"How does this team work with other Microsoft orgs day to day?"
"What did the last new grad on this team find hardest in their first 3 months?"
```

## Night-before checklist

- [ ] 8 stories in STAR(R), each with a Reflection line
- [ ] One story each for Respect, Integrity, Accountability
- [ ] Two growth-mindset stories (learned fast, changed after feedback)
- [ ] Strengths and weaknesses written and said out loud
- [ ] One Microsoft product I use, plus one improvement
- [ ] "Why Microsoft, why this team" with one sentence on long-term growth
- [ ] Every story fits in 2 minutes
- [ ] I will ask clarifying questions before every scenario and coding problem
- [ ] No AI tools open during the interview unless the interviewer says so
- [ ] Two questions ready for each interviewer
