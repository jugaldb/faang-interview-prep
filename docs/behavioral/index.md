# How behavioral interviews work

For interns, new grads, and engineers with 0 to 3 years of experience. You leave with the grading rubric, a timed STAR skeleton, and a 2-week plan that ends with 8 to 10 rehearsed stories.

## Why this round decides offers

- Steve Huynh, an ex-Amazon Principal Engineer and Bar Raiser, sat on nearly a thousand interview loops. He writes that candidates who missed offers "seldom failed because they lacked technical skill." His fix: if you plan 80 hours of prep, move 10 of them to stories ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/learnings-from-conducting-1000-interviews)).
- Hello Interview reports that most down-leveling decisions come from the behavioral round, not from technical gaps ([Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/how-behavioral-interviews-really-work)).
- At Amazon, about half of an SDE loop is Leadership Principle questions ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-interview-guide)). Each interviewer typically asks 2 or 3 of them ([Amazon SDE II prep](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/sde-ii-interview-prep)).
- When I was prepping for final rounds at Amazon and Meta, I asked friends at Google, Amazon, and Meta where strong candidates fail. The answer was the same: the behavioral round, not coding ([Why Smart Candidates Still Fail FAANG Interviews](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/why-smart-candidates-still-fail-faang)).

Do this now: put 10 to 12 hours of story prep on your calendar over the next 2 weeks, then follow [the 5-step prep plan](#the-5-step-prep-plan).

## How the round is scored

Interviewers walk in with a fixed list of competencies and a rubric. They write notes as plus and minus signals against each competency ([Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/how-behavioral-interviews-really-work)). Google scores answers on a written scale of poor, borderline, solid, and outstanding, and uses the same rubric for every candidate ([Google re:Work](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/a-guide-to-structured-interviewing-for-better-hiring-practices)).

### What the interviewer decides

| Decision | What drives it | What you control |
|---|---|---|
| Hire or no hire | Positive signal on nearly every competency tested | Have a story for every competency on the list |
| Level | Scope and difficulty of your stories | Lead with your biggest-scope stories |
| Credibility: do I believe them? | Specific details, numbers, honest mistakes | Use real stories with real numbers |
| Fit: would I work with them? | How you talk about others | Credit people, never blame, show you listened |
| Communication: can they structure thoughts? | Clear order, short answers | STAR, 2 to 3 minutes, answer the question asked |

Sources: [Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/how-behavioral-interviews-really-work) (the three implicit checks), [Tech Interview Handbook rubrics](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-rubrics/) (hire needs positive signal on nearly all areas for juniors).

One interviewer does not decide alone: a debrief or committee reviews all the notes ([Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-rubrics/)). At Google that is the [hiring committee](google-googleyness.md#hiring-committee), and at Amazon a [Bar Raiser](amazon-leadership-principles.md#the-bar-raiser) runs the debrief with the hiring manager.

### The 8 competencies most rubrics test

From the [Tech Interview Handbook rubric](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-rubrics/) by Lior Neu-ner, an ex-Meta engineering manager. A typical interview asks 5 or 6 questions and goes deep on each.

| Competency | Typical question | A strong junior answer shows |
|---|---|---|
| Motivation | Why this company? Why this team? | Specific reasons tied to the product and your past work |
| Proactivity | Tell me about something you did that nobody assigned. | You spotted a problem and acted without being asked |
| Working in an unstructured environment | Tell me about a project with unclear requirements. | You asked questions, wrote a plan, and checked in early |
| Perseverance | What did you stick with for months? | You kept going through setbacks and changed approach when needed |
| Conflict resolution | Tell me about a disagreement with a teammate. | You listened, used data, reached a decision, kept the relationship |
| Empathy | Tell me about helping a struggling teammate. | You understood their view and helped without taking over |
| Growth | Tell me about critical feedback you received. | You changed a behavior and can show the later result |
| Communication | Explain a technical topic to a non-engineer. | Short, clear, adjusted to the listener |

Do this: copy the 8 competencies into a doc and write one story idea next to each. Any row you leave blank is the first gap to fill in [Build your story bank](story-bank.md).

### What junior scope sounds like

| Level | Scope of impact in your stories | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Junior (intern, new grad, early career) | Your own work or your team's focus area, usually just you | [TIH rubrics](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview-rubrics/) |
| Senior | The whole team, three or more people | same |
| Staff | The org, two or more teams | same |

Hello Interview's example of a junior-level conflict is a code review debate about how to structure a class. Small scope is fine. Vague is not.

Then score each story on Huynh's four dimensions before you rehearse it ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/learnings-from-conducting-1000-interviews)):

| Dimension | Ask of your story | Entry-level target |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | How many people did my work affect? | Me, and it starts to help teammates |
| Contribution | What did I do, not the team? | Every action sentence starts with "I" |
| Impact | What changed, in numbers? | Time saved, bugs prevented, users, latency, grade |
| Difficulty | What made it hard? | A real constraint, trade-off, or obstacle |

### The four question types

| Type | Example | How to answer |
|---|---|---|
| Past behavior | Tell me about a time you missed a deadline. | One real story in STAR, 2 to 3 minutes |
| Open-ended | Walk me through the project you are most proud of. | Offer 2 or 3 options, let them pick, then STAR |
| Philosophy | How do you approach code reviews? | Your principle in one line, then one short real example |
| Hypothetical | What would you do if a teammate took credit for your work? | Clarify, state assumptions, compare options, choose, tie to a real example |

The first three types come from [Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/how-behavioral-interviews-really-work). Hypotheticals are common at Google; see [Googleyness and Leadership](google-googleyness.md#past-behavior-vs-hypothetical-questions).

## STAR, timed

STAR means Situation, Task, Action, Result. Amazon recommends it and also says it is not the only valid structure ([Amazon interview loop](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-loop)). Google's tips page lists the same four parts, with "Activity" in place of Action ([Google interview tips](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/interview-tips/)).

Amazon says a STAR answer typically runs 2 to 3 minutes ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-interview-guide)). MIT's career office splits the time 20/10/60/10 ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews/)). Applied to a 2.5-minute answer:

| Part | Share | Seconds | What to say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Situation | 20% | 30 | Where, when, your role, the stakes, one number |
| Task | 10% | 15 | What you owned and how success was measured |
| Action | 60% | 90 | 3 to 5 steps you took, each with a reason, all with "I" |
| Result | 10% | 15 | The number before and after, and who benefited |
| Learning (optional) | extra | 10 to 15 | One lesson and where you used it since |

Copy this skeleton for every story:

```text
Situation (30 s): In [month year], I was [role] on [team or project] at [place].
  [The problem, with one number]. [What happens if nobody fixes it].
Task (15 s): I owned [specific piece]. Success meant [measurable goal] by [date].
Action (90 s): First, I [step] because [reason]. Then I [step].
  The hard part was [obstacle], so I [what you did].
  I [worked with / convinced] [person or team] by [data or argument].
Result (15 s): [Metric] went from [before] to [after] in [time]. [Who benefited].
Learning (10 to 15 s): I learned [one lesson]. Since then I [habit],
  for example on [later project].
```

### Variants: pick one and use it every time

| Framework | Parts | When it helps | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| STAR | Situation, Task, Action, Result | Default for every company | [Amazon](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-loop), [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews/) |
| STAR-L | STAR plus Learning | Failure and feedback questions | [Rutgers](https://grad.rutgers.edu/news/bench-offer-mastering-art-interview) |
| STARI | STAR plus Improvement | Amazon; a Bar Raiser recommends ending with what you would improve | [About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/what-do-each-of-amazons-leadership-principles-really-mean) |
| CARL | Context, Action, Result, Learning | When your Situation and Task blur together | [University of Edinburgh](https://reflection.ed.ac.uk/reflectors-toolkit/reflecting-on-experience/carl), taught by [Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/behavioral/course/why-the-behavioral-matters) |
| SPSIL | Situation, Problem, Solution, Impact, Lesson | Same idea, IGotAnOffer's version | [IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/amazon-leadership-principles) |

### Five rules for every answer

1. **Say "I" for what you did.** Amazon's loop page asks you to focus on your individual contribution and say "I" instead of "we" ([Amazon](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-loop)). Keep "we" for the team's outcome.
    - Jugal's version: "'We built this feature' means nothing. 'I designed the caching layer, reducing latency by 40%', that's what they want to hear." ([Your 6-Week Amazon Interview Roadmap](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/amazon-is-still-hiring-after-the))
2. **Put a number in the result.** Use Google's formula: accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z] ([Google interview tips](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/interview-tips/)). If you have no exact number, give an honest estimate and say "roughly" ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/amazon-leadership-principles)), or use the ideas in [metrics when you have none](../resume/writing-bullets.md#metrics-when-you-have-none).
3. **Keep the setup under 30 seconds.** Time it ([IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/amazon-leadership-principles)).
4. **Answer the question asked.** A failure question needs a failure. A conflict question needs two people who disagreed.
5. **Stop and check in.** Amazon suggests pausing after your answer and asking whether the interviewer wants more context ([Amazon](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-loop)).

## The 5-step prep plan

Budget 10 to 12 hours over 2 weeks. That matches Huynh's suggestion of about 10 hours of story prep.

1. **Decode what your company scores (1 hour).** Open your company's page in the table below and the job description. Write down the 6 to 8 values or competencies you will be graded on. For Amazon, a Bar Raiser says the job description usually shows which principles matter most ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/what-do-each-of-amazons-leadership-principles-really-mean)).
2. **Collect 20 to 30 moments and keep 8 to 10 (3 hours).** Follow [Build your story bank](story-bank.md). It uses interviewing.io's selection method: brainstorm, scope, add evidence, filter, title ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/blog/stop-memorizing-star-for-behavioral-interviews-start-selecting-better-stories)).
3. **Write each story as a STAR card with a number (3 hours).** Use the [story card](story-bank.md#step-5-write-each-story-on-a-card). Map each story to 2 or 3 company values. Then do an "I" audit: replace every "we" that hides your own action.
4. **Rehearse out loud and record (2 hours).** Time each story, then have a friend ask "why?" and "what exactly did you do?" five times per story.
    - Jugal's rule: "Record yourself. Watch it back. Cringe. Do it again. The first time you tell these stories, they'll sound scripted. By the tenth time, they'll sound natural." ([Your 6-Week Amazon Interview Roadmap](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/amazon-is-still-hiring-after-the))
5. **Run 2 or 3 mocks and fix the weakest story (2 to 3 hours).** Book a free peer on [Aced Practice](https://www.aced.io/practice), do one AI session on [Free Mock Interview](https://freemockinterview.com/), or pay for one on [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/). Ask your partner to grade each answer as poor, borderline, solid, or outstanding. More options: [Mock interviews](../coding/mock-interviews.md).
    - After each mock, ask two of Jugal's review questions: "Did I talk enough?" and "Would you hire me based on this interview?"

### Two-week schedule

| Days | Do this | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Step 1: list what your company scores | 6 to 8 competencies on one page |
| 2 to 3 | Brainstorm moments from every source | 20 to 30 one-line moments |
| 4 to 5 | Score, filter, write 5 story cards | 5 STAR cards with numbers |
| 6 to 7 | Write 3 to 5 more cards, fill the mapping grid | 8 to 10 cards, grid with no empty rows |
| 8 to 10 | Rehearse 3 stories a day out loud, recorded | Each story at 2 to 3 minutes |
| 11 to 12 | Two mocks, one peer and one AI or paid | Notes on your 2 weakest answers |
| 13 | Rewrite the 2 weakest stories, re-record | Fixed stories |
| 14 | Prepare "Tell me about yourself", "Why [company]", and questions to ask | Three short scripts |

- [ ] I know the 6 to 8 competencies my target company scores.
- [ ] I have 8 to 10 stories, each with at least one number.
- [ ] Every row of my mapping grid has at least 2 stories.
- [ ] I have a real failure story with a lesson I applied later.
- [ ] Each story runs 2 to 3 minutes out loud.
- [ ] I recorded myself at least once per story.
- [ ] I did 2 mocks and fixed what they exposed.
- [ ] I have a 60 to 90 second "Tell me about yourself".
- [ ] I have 3 questions ready for each interviewer.

## Red flags that sink answers

| Red flag | What it sounds like | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blaming others | "The backend team kept breaking things." | Describe what you did and what you learned | [About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-job-interview-questions-tips-bar-raisers), [Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/how-behavioral-interviews-really-work) |
| Negative talk about an employer | "My last manager had no idea what he was doing." | Talk about the situation, not the person | [About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-job-interview-mistakes) |
| Vague or rambling | Five minutes of context, then "so yeah, it worked out" | 30-second setup, numbers, a clear end | [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/learnings-from-conducting-1000-interviews) |
| No numbers | "It made things a lot faster." | "From 40 minutes to 24 minutes" | [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/amazon-leadership-principles) |
| "We" everywhere | "We designed, we built, we shipped." | "I" for your actions, "we" for the outcome | [Amazon](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-loop) |
| No stakes | The interviewer can't tell why it mattered | One line on what happens if nobody acts | [Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/how-behavioral-interviews-really-work) |
| Too low-level too fast | Function names in the first 20 seconds | Start with the problem and the people, then go technical when asked | same |
| Scripted delivery | Word-perfect, flat, no pauses | Memorize bullets, not sentences | [Amazon worksheet](https://duaa2xs5z3ldn.cloudfront.net/assets/McsJv4WXKWb_FlRc_LRX21Ro-EvUopwEW-LeadershipPrinciplesSTARtemplate.pdf) |
| Asking "is that good enough?" after every answer | Seeking approval instead of offering detail | Ask once or twice, as an offer to go deeper | [Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/how-behavioral-interviews-really-work) |
| Arguing who was right | A conflict story that proves the other person wrong | Focus on how you reached a decision together | [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/amazon-leadership-principles) |
| Solo hero | "I carried the whole team." | Show how you worked with others | [Hello Interview Google L4](https://www.hellointerview.com/guides/google/l4) |
| Fake failure | "I work too hard." | A real miss with consequences and a lesson | [IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/tech/tell-me-about-a-time-you-failed) |
| Confidential details | Unreleased features, revenue numbers | Use public information or relative numbers | [About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-job-interview-mistakes) |
| Invented stories | A story built to fit the question | Use a real story or say you have not faced it | [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/amazon-leadership-principles) |

Do this: play back each recorded story and tick off every row above it hits. Fix those lines before the next take.

> **Watch out:** interviewing.io's advice on a missing story is that no answer is often better than a bad one. Use the bridge script below instead of inventing a story.

## Scripts for hard moments

```text
Mind goes blank (from Jugal's Amazon roadmap):
"Give me 30 seconds to think through this."

No story fits exactly (bridge; idea from Hello Interview):
"I haven't faced that exact situation. The closest was [situation],
where I [action]. Want me to walk through that one?"

Open-ended question (offer a menu; idea from Hello Interview):
"I could talk about [a technical trade-off], [a disagreement on my team],
or [a production bug I owned]. Which is most useful for you?"

Mid-story fork:
"From here I can go into the technical details or how I got the team
to agree. Which would help more?"

Lost the thread:
"Let me restate that more clearly. The short version is [one sentence]."

After a long answer (once or twice per interview; idea from Amazon's loop page):
"Is that the level of detail you want, or should I go deeper on any part?"
```

## Tell me about yourself

The Tech Interview Handbook lists it among the three questions to prepare first, and its [self-introduction guide](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/self-introduction/) says it is almost always asked first ([Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/)). Keep it to 60 to 90 seconds. Jugal's 6-step pitch (hook, introduce yourself, highlight value, align with the audience, call to action, practice) works here with small changes ([Craft the Elevator Pitch](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/craft-the-elevator-pitch-that-gets)).

```text
Present (20 s): I'm [name], a [year and degree] at [school] / [role] at [company].
  Most of my work is in [area].
Proof (30 s): Recently I [project or internship result with a number].
  Before that, I [second proof with a number].
Why here (20 s): I'm interested in [team or company] because [specific product,
  problem, or technology you have actually used or built with].
Hand-off (5 s): Happy to go deeper on any of those.
```

Write it, read it aloud with a timer, and cut until it fits 90 seconds. For the 30-second career fair version, see [Career fairs](../outreach/career-fairs.md).

## Questions to ask at the end

Prepare 3 per interviewer. Do not repeat the same question across a loop. Pick from the list below or from the [Tech Interview Handbook's final questions](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/final-questions/).

- What does success look like in this role in the first 90 days? ([Grow with Google](https://grow.google/grow-your-career/articles/interview-tips/))
- What are the biggest challenges the team faces now, and how would this role help? (same source)
- What is the code review and design review process like? ([awesome-behavioral-interviews](https://github.com/ashishps1/awesome-behavioral-interviews))
- What does onboarding look like for a new grad here? (same source)
- What is the most interesting project you have worked on here? (same source)
- At Amazon, ask about users: interviewing.io suggests framing your question around the customer experience to show Customer Obsession ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/guides/amazon-leadership-principles)).

## Use AI to prepare, never during the interview

Google states that AI tools are not permitted during its interviews ([Google: how we hire](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/how-we-hire/)). Use AI before, as a skeptical practice partner. Jugal's rule: "AI gives you a 70% draft fast, your job is the last 30% that makes it true and yours." ([The job-search tool stack I'd actually use in 2026](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-search-tool-stack-id-actually))

```text
You are a [Company] interviewer scoring a behavioral answer from a [intern / new grad]
software engineer. Here is my story in STAR form:
[paste story]

1. Ask me 5 follow-up questions, one at a time, like a skeptical interviewer.
2. After my answers, grade the story as poor, borderline, solid, or outstanding,
   and say why.
3. List every sentence where I said "we" but should say "I".
4. List every claim that needs a number.
5. Do not invent details or numbers. If something is missing, ask me for it.
```
