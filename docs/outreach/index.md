# Outreach strategy and order of operations

For students and early-career engineers who apply online and hear nothing back. Use it to decide whom to contact at each company, in what order, with which template, and how many messages to send a week.

## Why outreach works

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Referred candidates who reach an interview | 40%, vs 3% of inbound applicants and 25% of sourced candidates | [Ashby, 38M+ applications, 2021 to 2024](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals) |
| Referred candidates who interview and then reach the offer stage | 16% | Same Ashby report |
| Inbound offer rate | Fell from 0.7% (Q3 2021) to 0.2% (Q4 2024) as inbound application volume roughly tripled | Same Ashby report |
| Share of applications that are referrals | About 1% | Same Ashby report |
| How 503 job seekers who landed interviews got them | 14% employee referral, 13% a recruiter reached out, 12% networking, 62% applied online. Yet 85% of 555 respondents spent most of their search time on online applications. | [Huntr Q1 2026 survey](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q1-2026) |
| Channels ranked by about 500 engineers (mostly experienced) | Best: warm referrals, then in-house recruiters contacting you, then applying online, then cold email to hiring managers. Net negative: cold outreach to recruiters, cold referrals from strangers, agency recruiters | [interviewing.io part 1](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-to-get-in-the-door-at-top-companies-part-1) |

Jugal's version of the same data: "When I was searching for my first US role, I sent around 700 applications and got 3 interviews." He fixed it with people, not a better resume. He now gets 50+ recruiter messages a month, almost none from application portals ([7 videos on networking your way to offers](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to)).

What a referral does not do: "a referral mostly buys you one thing, a human actually reading your resume. You still have to earn the interview." (same post). In Ashby's data, 60% of referred applicants never reach an interview.

> **Watch out:** interviewing.io's sample was mostly experienced engineers (average about 8 years). The direction holds for students, but the exact ranking may differ. Ashby's numbers come from jobs on its own hiring platform, not from FAANG specifically.

## Before you send a single message

1. Check sponsorship if you need it. Jugal once got a referral and tailored everything, then heard "Unfortunately, we do not sponsor visas at this time" ([How to Check if a Company Sponsors H-1B Visas](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-to-check-if-a-company-sponsors)). Use the [USCIS H-1B Employer Data Hub](https://www.uscis.gov/tools/reports-and-studies/h-1b-employer-data-hub) and the steps on [international students](../jobs/international-students.md).
2. Finish your resume. Outreach multiplies whatever you send. Run the [resume checklist](../resume/checklist.md) and tailor one version per job ([tailoring](../resume/tailoring.md)).
3. Fix your LinkedIn profile. People click your profile before they reply ([LinkedIn profile](../linkedin/index.md)).
4. Build a target list of 20 to 30 companies, each with 1 to 3 live job IDs ([application strategy](../jobs/application-strategy.md); interns: [finding and applying](../internships/finding-and-applying.md)).
5. Set up a tracker with a row per person and a follow-up date ([follow-up and tracking](follow-up-and-tracking.md)).
6. Watch Jugal's video 1 today, then send one message. Spread the other six over 3 days ([video order](#jugals-7-video-order)).

## The order of operations for each company

Run this for every company on your list. It follows interviewing.io's channel data and Ashby's funnel data above.

1. Pick 1 to 3 live job IDs on the same team family. A specific ask beats a vague one: when Google asked employees specific questions such as "who is the best finance person you ever worked for", referrals rose by more than a third, while doubling the referral bonus did nothing ([LinkedIn Talent Blog on Laszlo Bock, 2015](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/how-google-dramatically-increased-referrals)).
2. Check the company's referral flow before you apply. At Microsoft the referral reportedly must come before your application (Simplify); at Meta the referral link expires after 30 days (Aced). Details on [referrals](referrals.md#how-it-works-at-google-meta-amazon-and-microsoft).
3. Contact warm people first: friends, ex-colleagues, classmates, TAs, club alumni. Ask for the referral directly with [R1](templates.md#r1-friend-or-ex-colleague).
4. If you have no warm contact, find 1 to 2 alumni or engineers on the team ([finding people](finding-people.md)). Ask for a 15-minute chat. Ask for the referral only after the chat.
5. In parallel, email the hiring manager of the exact team ([cold email](cold-email.md)).
6. Apply on the careers site the same day you get the referral link, or the same day you apply anyway if no referral is coming.
7. Within 1 to 3 days of applying, send the university or technical recruiter a short note with the job ID ([E1](templates.md#e1-university-recruiter-after-you-apply) or [E2](templates.md#e2-technical-recruiter-after-you-apply)).
8. Leave cold referral asks to strangers for last, and only with real common ground. interviewing.io found cold referrals net negative at FAANG: "Given that cold referrals aren't zero effort, our advice is to expend that energy elsewhere."
9. Follow up at most twice per person, then stop ([follow-ups](templates.md#follow-ups)).
10. Log every touch in your tracker.

## How much to send each week

| Mode | Volume | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum | 5 new conversations a week, which is 20 a month | 30 minutes a week | Jugal: "One message does almost nothing. Five a week for a month is 20 conversations started." ([7 videos on networking](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to)); 30-minute weekly ritual ([The One Skill post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-one-skill-that-can-unlock-every)) |
| Active search | 10 to 15 personalized emails a day | A 10-minute block every day | Jugal ([The Job Hunt I Didn't Burn Out Doing](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-i-didnt-burn-out-doing)) |
| Introvert mode | 2 to 3 people at a time | Plan each one | Jugal: pick 2 to 3 people instead of 20, listen more, ask good questions ([The One Skill post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-one-skill-that-can-unlock-every)) |

Hard limits that apply in every mode:

1. Contact 1 to 2 people per company per wave ([Hunter 2026](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email)).
2. Send at most 3 touches per person: 1 message and 2 follow-ups (same Hunter report; Jugal: "follow up once. Maybe twice max.").
3. Write one personal line by hand in every message. Hunter's data: manual edits raised replies 18%, two custom details raised them 56%, and 69% of decision makers said it bothers them if AI was used.
4. Spend your 3 to 5 free LinkedIn personalized notes a month on your top targets ([finding people](finding-people.md#stay-inside-linkedins-limits)).

A suggested week for an active search:

| Day | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Find and verify 10 new people across 5 to 10 companies | 45 minutes |
| Tuesday to Thursday | Send 3 to 5 first messages a day, each with one hand-written line | 15 minutes a day |
| Friday | Send due follow-ups, log replies, book calls for next week | 30 minutes |
| Any day | Same-day thank-you after every call; referral ask if the call went well | 10 minutes |

## When to send

1. Send on a weekday morning in the recipient's time zone. Belkins studied 7.5 million B2B cold emails sent in 2025: reply rates were nearly equal across weekdays (0.44% to 0.48%) and highest for sends between 8 AM and 12 PM (0.54%) ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/best-time-to-send-email)). Jugal's own tip is 8:00 AM ([I Used AI to Apply for 100+ Jobs](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/i-used-ai-to-apply-for-100-jobs-without)).
2. Do not wait for the perfect day. Decision makers told Hunter that "it doesn't matter if it's a Monday at 3 PM" ([Hunter State of Cold Email 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)).
3. Write at night, then use Gmail's Schedule send (up to 100 scheduled emails) to deliver in the morning ([Gmail Help: schedule emails](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/9214606)).
4. Time referral asks to postings. Jugal: "the posted deadline is almost never the real one"; reviews are rolling ([494 Summer 2027 Internships Are Already Live](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/494-summer-2027-internships-are-already)).

## Weak vs strong first messages

| Weak | Strong | Why the strong one works |
|---|---|---|
| "Hi, I hope this email finds you well. I am a hardworking student passionate about technology. Can you refer me to any role at Google?" | "Hi Arjun, fellow Purdue CS here. I'm applying to Google for SWE new grad roles and would love to hear how you went from Purdue to Cloud Storage. Open to connecting?" | Shared school, one specific role, a small ask (a chat), under 200 characters |
| "Please find my resume attached. Kindly let me know if there are openings." | "I applied to SDE Intern (ID 3012345) on October 2. Campus-store payments app: 400 orders a week, zero double charges. Is there anything else you need from me this cycle?" | Job ID, one proof with a number, one question |
| "Can you refer me? Here is my resume." (to a stranger) | "Thanks again for the time on Tuesday. What you said about testing changed how I'm approaching the role. I've applied to ID 8412233. Here's my resume and a 3-line summary you can paste." | Comes after a conversation, references it, makes the referral a 60-second task |

## Mistakes that cost replies

- Messaging recruiters before you apply. They need an application with a job ID to find you.
- Asking for "any role". Name one job ID.
- Sending cold referral asks to dozens of strangers. Talk first ([referrals](referrals.md)).
- Attaching a resume to a first cold email to a hiring manager. interviewing.io says skip the attachment and include 1 to 2 links instead.
- Opening with "I hope this email finds you well" and a life story. Jugal: most cold emails "die in the first two lines" because of it ([7 videos on networking](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to)).
- Following up 5 times. Three touches total, then stop.
- Mass mail merges with no personal line. See the Hunter numbers above.
- Spending a referral on a company that does not sponsor, when you need sponsorship.
- Letting a referral link expire. Meta's link lasts 30 days, according to Aced ([referrals](referrals.md#how-it-works-at-google-meta-amazon-and-microsoft)).
