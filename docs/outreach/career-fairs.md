# Career fairs and the 30-second pitch

For students going to a campus fair, a virtual fair, or a conference expo. You leave with a target list, a 30-second pitch, three questions per booth, and follow-ups ready to send within 24 hours.

My first internship came from a virtual career fair in my second year. I sent the speaker a short thank-you on LinkedIn, they replied, that led to a call, the call led to an introduction, and three months later I had the internship ([The One Skill That Can Unlock Every Opportunity](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-one-skill-that-can-unlock-every)). Plan the [follow-up](#after-the-fair) before you plan the booths.

## Get the trip paid for

Conference passes, flights, and hotels add up. Two kinds of money exist: conference scholarships (often registration, sometimes hotel) and company travel grants (often flights and hotel). Jugal's guide lists 30 programs and a 5-step method ([How Students Can Find and Apply for Conference Scholarships](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-students-can-find-and-apply-for)).

1. Check eligibility on each program page. Drop the ones you do not qualify for.
2. Put every deadline in one list. Set a reminder two weeks before the earliest.
3. Write one base answer each for: why you want to attend, what you hope to gain, why you deserve the funding. Keep your CV current.
4. Tailor each application with the prompt below.
5. Submit early and stack awards where allowed (a university travel grant for flights plus a conference scholarship for registration).

Example: the [Citadel Conference Travel Grant](https://www.citadel.com/careers/programs-and-events/conference-travel-grant/) covers round-trip airfare, lodging, airport transport, a meal stipend, and a full-access pass (except at GHC), plus Citadel recruiting dinners. As of Oct 4, 2026 the GHC 2026 round had closed (Sept 25) and the NeurIPS 2026 round closed Oct 5 ([Harvard summary](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/blog/2026/07/23/citadel-conference-travel-grant/)). Put next year's rounds in your deadline list now.

Jugal's mentor prompt, verbatim from the post:

```text
You are an expert scholarship application mentor who has helped students win conference scholarships and travel grants.

Interview me step by step before writing anything.

Identify gaps in my profile and suggest realistic improvements.

Then help me prepare:

- Essays
- Personal statement
- Resume
- LinkedIn profile
- Project descriptions
- Achievement summaries
- Networking introduction
- Common application questions

Rewrite everything to sound authentic, specific, and memorable without exaggerating my achievements.

Be brutally honest if any part of my application is weak.
```

## Prep timeline

### 3 to 4 weeks before

- [ ] Register on Handshake for the campus fair, or buy the conference ticket and apply for grants.
- [ ] Copy the attending employer list. Pick 10 targets: 5 must-visit and 5 warm-ups.
- [ ] For each target, find 1 to 3 open roles on the company's careers site and note the job IDs. Recruiters may not know every job on their own site, so naming the exact role keeps the talk specific ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-career-fair-phd-postdoc-guide/)).
- [ ] International students: note each company's sponsorship status ([how to check](../jobs/international-students.md)).
- [ ] Apply online to roles that are already live, so the recruiter has a record to find.
- [ ] Conferences: upload your resume to the attendee or talent database.

### 1 to 2 weeks before

- [ ] Tailor a one-page resume to your main role family ([resume checklist](../resume/checklist.md)).
- [ ] Print 15 to 20 copies. Keep the PDF on your phone and a QR code to your LinkedIn.
- [ ] Update LinkedIn. Reps look you up after the fair ([LinkedIn profile](../linkedin/index.md)).
- [ ] Write your pitch (next section). Record it 3 times and time each take.
- [ ] Write 3 questions per must-visit company.
- [ ] Fill the target list below.

### The day before

- [ ] Map your route through the hall from the floor plan.
- [ ] Charge your phone. Pack a pen, a folder, water, and a snack.
- [ ] Re-read your target list and one recent fact about each company.

### Target list template

Paste into a sheet, one row per company:

```text
Priority | Company | Booth | Role + Job ID | Applied (Y/N) | Sponsors (Y/N/?) | My hook (their product or team) | Question 1 | Question 2 | Question 3 | Rep name | Rep email or LinkedIn | What they said | Next step | Follow-up sent (date)
```

## The 30-second pitch formula

Jugal's six steps, from [Craft the Elevator Pitch That Gets You Hired at Career Fairs](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/craft-the-elevator-pitch-that-gets):

| Step | What to say | Jugal's example (verbatim) | Plain student version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | A question, fact, or bold statement from your field | "Ever wondered how top apps maintain lightning-fast performance even with millions of users? That's where I come in." | "Last semester I got our club's app from 200 to 2,000 users without it slowing down." |
| 2. Introduce yourself | Who you are and what you do | "Hi, I'm Jugal, a master's student in software engineering, specializing in scalable applications." | "I'm [Name], a [junior] in CS at [School]." |
| 3. Highlight your value | One skill or result, with a number | "In my last role, I optimized a backend service that reduced load times by 40%, enhancing the user experience for millions." | "At [internship], I [did X], which [result with a number]." |
| 4. Align with the audience | Tie it to their team | "I noticed your team is focusing on enhancing user engagement, and I'd love to contribute my expertise in building responsive and efficient software." | "I saw your [team] is hiring [role], and that's the stack I've been working in." |
| 5. Call to action | Ask for the next step | "I'd appreciate the chance to discuss how I can contribute to your next big project. Could we schedule a time to chat?" | "What does a strong intern on [team] work on in the first month? Who's the best person to follow up with?" |
| 6. Practice | Rehearse until it sounds natural | | Record 3 takes on your phone and time each |

The formula in one line:

```text
[Hook: one result with a number] + [Name, year, school] + [What you built and with what] + [Why their team or role] + [One question]
```

Rules:

1. Keep it to 30 seconds. MIT's guide allows 30 to 60 seconds and says to end with a question that engages the listener ([MIT CAPD pitch toolkit](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-prep-your-elevator-pitch-in-a-flash/)). At a busy booth, use 30.
2. Write about 70 to 80 words. At a calm pace of about 150 words a minute, that is 30 seconds. Time it to be sure.
3. Use a plain hook with a number. It is easier to say naturally than a dramatic question.
4. One number, one project. Recruiters remember one thing.
5. End with a question, so it becomes a conversation.

### Weak vs strong

Weak:

```text
Hi, I'm Rahul. I'm a CS student. I'm really passionate about technology and I'm a
fast learner and a team player. I know Python, Java, C++, React and SQL. Are you
hiring? Here's my resume.
```

Strong (about 70 words):

```text
Hi, I'm Rahul, a junior in CS at Purdue. Last semester I built a course-planner
app that 1,200 students used during registration week, mostly in Python and
Postgres. I saw your Summer 2027 SWE Intern role lists backend APIs, which is what
I've been working in. What does a strong intern on your payments team work on in
the first month? And is there anything I should do after today besides applying?
```

### Pitch templates

Intern:

```text
Hi, I'm [Name], a [sophomore/junior] in CS at [School]. Last semester I built [project] that [result with a number], mostly in [language/stack]. I saw your [Summer 2027 SWE Intern] role lists [skill from the job description], which is what I've been working in. What does a strong intern on [team] spend their first month on? And is there anything I should do after today besides applying?
```

New grad:

```text
Hi, I'm [Name], graduating in [Month Year] with a [degree] from [School]. At [internship] I [shipped X] that [result with a number]. I'm applying to your new grad [SWE] role ([Job ID]) because [one specific reason about their product]. Which teams are hiring new grads this cycle, and what separates the candidates who move forward?
```

Engineer with 0 to 3 years of experience:

```text
Hi, I'm [Name], a [backend] engineer at [Company] for [N] years. I own [system], and last quarter I [result with a number]. I'm looking at [role] on your [team] because [specific reason]. What level are you hiring at for that team, and who should I follow up with?
```

International student (add as your third question):

```text
One question that matters for my planning: does this role support candidates on F-1 OPT and STEM OPT?
```

Typed opener for a virtual fair chat (about 50 words):

```text
Hi [Name], I'm [Name], a [year] in [major] at [School]. I built [project] ([result with a number]) and applied to [role] ([Job ID]) yesterday. Which team is hiring for it this cycle, and what do you look for in candidates who move forward? My LinkedIn: [link]
```

The same pitch answers "Tell me about yourself" in interviews. See [behavioral interviews](../behavioral/index.md).

### Practice drill

1. Write your pitch from a template. Cut it to 80 words or fewer.
2. Say it out loud with a timer. Cut until it fits in 30 seconds.
3. Record 3 takes on your phone. Note filler words ("basically", "like") and remove them.
4. Have a friend interrupt you at 15 seconds with a question. Real reps interrupt.
5. Use it at 2 warm-up booths before your top targets.
6. For weekly practice, visit a [Toastmasters](https://www.toastmasters.org/) club as a free guest (membership is paid), or watch 2 talks from the [TED talks on public speaking](https://www.ted.com/playlists/226/ted_talks_on_public_speaking) playlist.

## At the booth: three questions, then leave

MIT's method limits each conversation to three questions ([MIT CAPD career fair guide](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-career-fair-phd-postdoc-guide/)):

1. **Pitch plus your first question**, about a specific role or project.
2. **A fit question.** For example: does the team hire for existing skills, or train people into the role?
3. **The question that matters to you**, such as visa sponsorship.

Then close:

1. Ask for the rep's name and the best way to follow up (email or LinkedIn) ([Michigan Tech fair prep](https://www.mtu.edu/career/students/career-fair-prep/index.html)).
2. Ask what the next step is.
3. Offer your resume if they want it. Many will say "apply online"; you already did.
4. Thank them and step aside. There is a line behind you.
5. Write three lines of notes before the next booth.

```text
[Company] | [Rep name, title] | [email or LinkedIn]
Said: [one specific thing they told you]
Next: [apply to Job ID / email them by date / attend info session]
```

| Ask this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "What is [team] hiring interns for this cycle?" | "So what does your company do?" |
| "What separates candidates who move to interviews?" | "Can you get me an interview?" |
| "Is there anything I should do after today besides applying?" | "Do you have any jobs for me?" |
| "Does this role support F-1 OPT and STEM OPT?" (third question) | Opening the conversation with visa status |
| "Who's the best person to follow up with?" | Leaving without a name |

## What to bring

- [ ] 15 to 20 printed resumes in a folder (one page, no staples needed).
- [ ] Your phone with the resume PDF, a LinkedIn QR code, and your target list.
- [ ] A pen and a small notebook, or a notes app ready.
- [ ] Clothes one step more formal than the company's office. Comfortable shoes; fairs mean hours of standing.
- [ ] A charged phone and a battery pack for conference days.
- [ ] Your pitch and 3 questions per target, on one card.

## Virtual fairs

1. Book 1:1 or group slots as soon as the schedule opens on Handshake or the company's event page.
2. Test your camera, mic, and lighting the day before. Sit in a quiet room.
3. Keep your pitch and 2 questions on a sticky note beside the camera.
4. In group sessions, use the chat: paste the typed opener above with your job ID and LinkedIn.
5. Note the names of speakers and reps. Screenshot the participant list where that is allowed; Jugal's networking guide suggests it for follow-ups ([Holy Grail of Networking](https://jugaldb.notion.site/The-Holy-Grail-of-Networking-A-Z-with-templates-1a0af2117b838027aa5cd47911f2a20f)).
6. Send a thank-you to the speaker or rep within 24 hours.

## Conference expos

1. Upload your resume to the conference database before you travel.
2. Treat on-site interviews as real interviews. SHPE's page promises interviews and job offers on site. Review the [interview framework](../coding/interview-framework.md) and your [story bank](../behavioral/story-bank.md) before you fly.
3. Keep an afternoon block free each day for interviews that get scheduled at the booth.
4. Go to company talks and info sessions, not just the expo floor. Thank any speaker whose talk you can name a detail from.
5. Visit 1 or 2 low-priority booths first to warm up, then your top 5.
6. Read the [company guide](../companies/index.md) for each top-5 target so you know its interview format before you talk to them.

## International students

1. Check sponsorship before the fair, so you do not spend your best minutes on a company that cannot hire you ([international students](../jobs/international-students.md)).
2. Ask about sponsorship as your third question, after you have made your case.
3. For defense and aerospace employers, search the posting for "export control", "US person", and "sponsorship". Export control roles usually require US person status: citizen, green card holder, refugee, or asylee ([494 Summer 2027 Internships](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/494-summer-2027-internships-are-already)).
4. Send your fair follow-ups the same night. On F-1 or OPT your search clock is shorter, and a warm intro is the fastest way to buy back time ([7 videos on networking](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to)).

## After the fair

1. **Within 24 hours,** send a thank-you email or LinkedIn request with one specific detail from your conversation ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-prep-your-elevator-pitch-in-a-flash/)).
2. **The same night,** apply to any role the rep pointed you to and put the job ID in your note.
3. **Log it** in your tracker with a follow-up date 7 business days out ([follow-up and tracking](follow-up-and-tracking.md)).
4. **A month later,** a further follow-up with a real question, or a request for a 30-minute informational chat with someone on the team, is fine ([MIT CAPD career fair guide](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-career-fair-phd-postdoc-guide/)).

Follow-up email:

```text
Subject: Great meeting you at [Fair], [Your Name], [School]

Hi [Name],

Thanks for talking with me at the [Company] booth at [Fair] [yesterday]. I enjoyed hearing about [specific thing they said].

As you suggested, I applied to [role] (Job ID [ID]). My resume is attached. [One line linking your project to what they said.]

Thanks again,
[Name]
[LinkedIn]
```

LinkedIn note (under 200 characters, the limit for a personalized invitation). Free accounts get 3 to 5 personalized notes a month (LinkedIn's help pages give both numbers: [a563153](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a563153), [a550555](https://www.linkedin.com/help/billing/answer/a550555)), so spend them on the reps you most want to hear from:

```text
Hi [Name], thanks for the chat at the [Company] booth at [Fair] about [topic]. I applied to [role] ([Job ID]) tonight. Would love to stay connected.
```

Thank-you to a speaker:

```text
Hi [Name], thank you for your talk at [event] on [topic]. [One line on what you will do with it.] I'm a [year] at [School] working on [area]. Would love to stay connected.
```

More templates: [templates](templates.md). Writing rules for longer follow-ups: [cold email](cold-email.md).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Walking the floor with no list | 10 targets, ranked, with job IDs |
| A 2-minute life story | 30 seconds, one project, one number, one question |
| "What does your company do?" | Research one product or team before you arrive |
| Leaving without a name | Ask for the rep's name and best contact every time |
| Relying on the paper resume | Apply online the same night so your application is on record |
| Waiting a week to follow up | Within 24 hours, with one detail they said |
| Visiting only the biggest booths | Plan time for smaller companies and company talks too |
| Asking about visas first | Make your case first; ask as the third question |
