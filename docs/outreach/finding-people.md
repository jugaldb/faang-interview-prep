# Find the right people and their emails

For anyone with a target company list but no names. Finish with 1 to 2 named people per company, each with a verified email or a LinkedIn route, logged in your tracker.

## Who to find at each company

| Target | Title keywords to search | Where they show up | Use them for |
|---|---|---|---|
| University recruiter | university recruiter, campus recruiter, early career recruiter, university recruiting, emerging talent | LinkedIn, Handshake, career fair booths | Interns and new grads: status, timeline, events |
| Technical recruiter | technical recruiter, technical sourcer, talent acquisition | LinkedIn, inbound InMails | 0 to 3 years: status and process after you apply |
| Hiring manager | engineering manager, software development manager (Amazon's SDM), software engineering manager | LinkedIn, the job post's hiring team, engineering blogs, talks | A 15-minute chat; cold email works best here |
| Engineer on the team | software engineer, SDE, SWE, plus the team's keyword | LinkedIn, GitHub, engineering blogs, talks | Advice first, referral after a real conversation |
| Alumni at the company | your school plus the company | LinkedIn Alumni tool | Warm chat, then referral |
| Past interns | your school plus "intern" plus the company | LinkedIn Alumni tool | Intern referrals and what the loop felt like |
| Professors, TAs, lab managers | your department | Department and lab pages | Research roles and intros to industry |

Who to contact first, and for what, is on the [outreach strategy page](index.md). Target 1 to 2 people per company per wave: Hunter's 2026 data on 31 million emails found contacting 1 to 2 people per company got a 46% higher reply rate than contacting more ([Hunter State of Email Outreach 2026](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email)).

## LinkedIn people search, step by step

1. Type any keyword in the LinkedIn search bar and press Enter, then click the **People** tab.
2. Open **All filters** and set **Current company** to your target. This is more reliable than typing the company name as a keyword.
3. Paste a search string from the next section into the search box.
4. Add filters: **Locations** (the team's city), **Connections** (start with 2nd), and **School** (your school) for warm angles.
5. Run each search once and open only the profiles you would actually message. People searches count toward LinkedIn's monthly commercial use limit ([stay inside LinkedIn's limits](#stay-inside-linkedins-limits)).
6. For each person, write down name, title, team, and one specific thing they built, wrote, or posted. Jugal's tip: "before you write anything, find one specific thing the person built, wrote, or posted" ([7 videos on networking](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to)).
7. Log them in your tracker ([follow-up and tracking](follow-up-and-tracking.md)) before you send anything.

Search operator rules from LinkedIn ([LinkedIn Help: Boolean search](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a524335)):

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Operators must be uppercase: AND, OR, NOT | `recruiter AND Google` |
| Quotes for exact phrases | `"university recruiter"` |
| Parentheses to group | `(SDE OR "software engineer") AND payments` |
| Order of evaluation: quotes, then parentheses, then NOT, then AND, then OR | Wrap every OR group in parentheses |
| Not supported: wildcards (*), braces, brackets, angle brackets | `engineer*` does not work |
| Plus and minus are not officially supported | Use AND and NOT instead |

> **Tip:** Free accounts accept fewer operators per query, so keep strings short and split long ones in two ([100hires: LinkedIn Boolean search](https://100hires.com/linkedin-boolean-search.html)).

## Copy-paste LinkedIn search strings

Set **Current company** to the target first, then paste one of these into the search box.

University and campus recruiters (interns and new grads):

```text
("university recruiter" OR "campus recruiter" OR "early career recruiter" OR "university recruiting" OR "emerging talent")
```

Technical recruiters (0 to 3 years):

```text
("technical recruiter" OR "technical sourcer" OR "talent acquisition") AND (engineering OR software)
```

Recruiters who mention early careers anywhere on their profile:

```text
(recruiter OR "talent acquisition") AND ("new grad" OR university OR "early career" OR intern)
```

Hiring managers:

```text
("engineering manager" OR "software development manager" OR SDM OR "software engineering manager")
```

Engineers on a specific team (swap in the team's keywords from the job post):

```text
("software engineer" OR SDE OR SWE) AND (payments OR ledger)
```

Past interns from your school (also set the **School** filter):

```text
(intern OR internship) AND ("software engineer" OR SDE OR SWE)
```

Remove agency recruiters from any recruiter search:

```text
("technical recruiter" OR "university recruiter") NOT (agency OR staffing OR consultant)
```

India campus hiring teams:

```text
("campus hiring" OR "university hiring" OR "campus recruiter" OR "talent acquisition") AND (India OR Bengaluru OR Hyderabad)
```

UK and Europe graduate recruiters:

```text
("graduate recruiter" OR "early careers" OR "campus recruiter") AND (London OR Dublin OR Zurich)
```

Jugal's strings, from his [Holy Grail of Networking guide](https://jugaldb.notion.site/The-Holy-Grail-of-Networking-A-Z-with-templates-1a0af2117b838027aa5cd47911f2a20f) and [How I got 47 recruiter messages within a month](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-i-got-47-recruiter-messages-within). Use these without the Current company filter, since the company is in the string:

```text
Finding Recruiters:        ("Talent Acquisition" OR Recruiter OR "HR Manager") AND [Company Name]
Finding Hiring Managers:   ("Hiring Manager" OR "Engineering Manager" OR "VP Engineering") AND [Industry]
Finding Alumni:            [Your University] AND ("Software Engineer" OR Developer) AND [Target Company]
Finding by Seniority:      ("Senior" OR "Lead" OR "Principal") AND [Job Title] AND [Company/Industry]
People posting they hire:  "Software Engineer" AND "Google" AND "Hiring"
```

> **Tip:** For interns and new grads, a university recruiter beats a general technical recruiter. University or early-career teams usually own intern and new grad pipelines.

## Use the job post itself

1. Open the job on LinkedIn. If it shows **Meet the hiring team**, you can connect with and message everyone listed there, including the job poster ([LinkedIn Help: jobs through social hiring](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a767235)). Use template [E3](templates.md#e3-jugals-message-for-the-linkedin-hiring-team-button) or [C5](templates.md#c5-hiring-manager-after-you-apply).
2. In LinkedIn Jobs, turn on the **Hiring in your network** filter to see jobs posted by your connections (same help page). Those posters are your warmest contacts.
3. Do Jugal's fresh-post move: filter LinkedIn jobs to roles posted in the last 3 days, then message the team lead before the crowd finds the post ([The Job Hunt I Didn't Burn Out Doing](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-i-didnt-burn-out-doing)). Template: [H4](templates.md#h4-jugals-message-to-a-team-lead-about-a-fresh-post).
4. Read the job description for the team or org name (for example "Payments Infrastructure" or "AWS Lambda"). Search that name with the Current company filter to find the manager and engineers.
5. Note the job ID now. Every message you send later carries it.

## Use the LinkedIn Alumni tool

Alumni are the warmest strangers you have. Shared school is your common ground. Steps from [LinkedIn Help: the Alumni tab](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a567083):

1. Search your university's name on LinkedIn and open its school page (not the group).
2. Click **Alumni** in the left-side navigation.
3. In **Where they work**, type the target company.
4. In **What they do**, pick Engineering (or Information Technology).
5. In **What they studied**, pick Computer Science or your major.
6. Type a keyword such as `intern` or `new grad` in the search box on that page to surface recent grads. LinkedIn removed the graduation-year filter from the Alumni tab, so keywords are the workaround.
7. Sort your list: same degree program first, then same clubs or labs, then people who joined the company in the last 1 to 3 years (closest to your situation).
8. Send template [C4](templates.md#c4-alumni) (under 200 characters). After they accept, send [A1](templates.md#a1-chat-request-after-they-accept) or [A5](templates.md#a5-jugals-alumni-message-long-version).

## Google X-ray search

X-ray search means using Google to search public LinkedIn profiles. Use it when LinkedIn's own search hits its monthly limit. Rules:

1. No space after the colon: `site:linkedin.com/in` works, `site: linkedin.com/in` does not ([Google Search Help: refine searches](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/2466433)).
2. Put titles in quotes. Use a minus sign to exclude a word.
3. Use a country subdomain to narrow by country, such as `site:uk.linkedin.com/in` or `site:in.linkedin.com/in` ([100hires: LinkedIn X-ray search](https://100hires.com/linkedin-xray-search.html)).
4. Expect stale results. LinkedIn narrowed what Google indexes in early 2024, and public profiles take weeks or months to refresh in search engines (same 100hires guide). Confirm the person's current role on LinkedIn before you write.

Copy-paste strings (swap the company, city, and school):

```text
site:linkedin.com/in ("university recruiter" OR "campus recruiter" OR "early career recruiter") "Google"
```

```text
site:linkedin.com/in ("engineering manager" OR "software development manager") "Amazon" "Seattle" -recruiter
```

```text
site:linkedin.com/in "Purdue University" "Meta" ("software engineer" OR SWE)
```

```text
site:linkedin.com/in intitle:"technical recruiter" "Stripe"
```

```text
site:in.linkedin.com/in ("campus hiring" OR "university hiring" OR "talent acquisition") "Microsoft" "Hyderabad"
```

```text
site:uk.linkedin.com/in ("graduate recruiter" OR "early careers") "Bloomberg" "London"
```

> **Watch out:** `intitle:` and `OR` work in practice but are not on Google's documented operator list. If a string returns nothing, remove one part at a time.

## Find the email address

Hiring managers "live in their emails", so email beats LinkedIn for them ([interviewing.io part 2](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-to-get-in-the-door-at-top-companies-cold-out-reach-to-hiring-managers-part-2)). Recruiters and engineers you can usually reach on LinkedIn.

1. Write down the person's full name exactly as on LinkedIn.
2. Find the company's email domain. Check the careers site footer, the press page, or a recruiter's email signature. Use the domain (`stripe.com`), not the company name.
3. Run [Hunter Email Finder](https://hunter.io/email-finder): enter first name, last name, and domain. It checks a database of 100 million+ public addresses, then infers the likely pattern, and shows sources and a confidence score. Failed searches are free.
4. If Hunter finds nothing, try one backup from the tools table below.
5. If every tool fails, guess with the patterns below, then verify each guess.
6. Verify every address before sending. Use the decision table below.
7. Log the address, the tool, and the verification status in your tracker.

### Guess the pattern

1. Find one real address at the domain first. Search Google for the domain in quotes plus a work word, for example `"@stripe.com" engineer`, and check papers, conference speaker pages, and open-source READMEs written by the company's engineers. Copy that format.
2. If you find nothing, try interviewing.io's three fallbacks (firstname@, first initial + lastname@, firstname.lastname@), then the common variants below ([interviewing.io part 2](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-to-get-in-the-door-at-top-companies-cold-out-reach-to-hiring-managers-part-2)).
3. Verify each guess in [Hunter Email Verifier](https://hunter.io/email-verifier) before you send. Stop at the first Valid result.

Example person: Priya Shah at example.com.

| Order | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | first | priya@example.com |
| 2 | first initial + last | pshah@example.com |
| 3 | first.last | priya.shah@example.com |
| 4 | firstlast | priyashah@example.com |
| 5 | first_last | priya_shah@example.com |
| 6 | first + last initial | priyas@example.com |

> **Watch out:** Guessing is unreliable at big tech. Third-party pattern databases disagree even for Google and Amazon ([Prospeo on google.com](https://prospeo.io/c/google-com), [Clay on Amazon](https://clay.com/dossier/amazon-email-format)), and large companies often use usernames rather than full names. If no guess verifies, reach the person on LinkedIn instead.

### Decide what to send

| Verifier result | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Valid | The mailbox accepts mail | Send |
| Accept-all (catch-all) | The domain accepts everything, so the check cannot confirm the person | Run it through Truelist or MailTester.com. Still unsure: use LinkedIn instead. |
| Invalid | The mailbox does not exist | Drop it. Try the next pattern. |
| Unknown or timeout | The server did not answer | Do not send. Try again in a day or use LinkedIn. |

> **Tip:** Bounces hurt your sender reputation. Hunter's 2026 data puts the average bounce rate at 3.6% ([Hunter 2026](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email)). Never send to an unverified guess in bulk.

## Rules: privacy, law, and safety

1. Email one person at a time, with your real name, an honest subject line, and a real reason tied to them.
2. Stop the moment someone asks you to. Never re-add them to a later batch.
3. Send from your personal Gmail or your university address. Do not buy a new domain for outreach: Gmail requires SPF or DKIM authentication from every sender and expects a low spam rate ([Gmail sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126)), and a brand-new domain has no reputation.
4. Stay far below Gmail's sending cap. A personal Gmail account is blocked for 1 to 24 hours after more than 500 emails in a day ([Gmail sending limits](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/22839)). Jugal's own pace is 10 to 15 emails a day ([The Job Hunt I Didn't Burn Out Doing](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-i-didnt-burn-out-doing)).
5. Do not harvest emails from GitHub commits. Many are private noreply addresses anyway ([GitHub Docs: commit email](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/how-tos/email-preferences/setting-your-commit-email-address)).
6. Do not use downloaded "HR contact" spreadsheets or PDFs. They hold other people's personal data, and they go stale fast.
7. US: the CAN-SPAM Act covers email whose primary purpose is advertising a commercial product or service ([FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). A one-to-one job inquiry is likely outside it, but follow its habits anyway: real name, honest subject, honor opt-outs. This is not legal advice.
8. UK: the ICO says the PECR email marketing rule does not apply to corporate subscribers, but UK GDPR still applies to an email that identifies a person. Do not hide your identity, give a way to opt out, and respect objections ([ICO: business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)). This is not legal advice.
9. Check any recruiter who contacts you. Find the role on the company's official careers site and match the email domain. "Honest employers, including the federal government, will never ask you to pay to get a job" ([FTC: job scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams)).

## Build your contact list

1. List 20 to 30 target companies, each with 1 to 3 live job IDs ([application strategy](../jobs/application-strategy.md)).
2. International students: check sponsorship before you find a single contact ([international students](../jobs/international-students.md)). Jugal once got a referral, tailored everything, and then heard "Unfortunately, we do not sponsor visas at this time" ([How to Check if a Company Sponsors H-1B Visas](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-to-check-if-a-company-sponsors)).
3. For each company, find 1 to 2 people using the order on the [strategy page](index.md#who-to-contact-for-what).
4. Record these columns for each person: Company, Role, Job ID, Contact name, Contact type (recruiter, HM, engineer, alumni, friend), Channel, Email, Verification status, LinkedIn URL, One specific thing about them, First touch date, Follow-up dates, Replied, Next action. A full tracker template is on [follow-up and tracking](follow-up-and-tracking.md).
5. Check the company page for that company's process before you write, for example [Google](../companies/google.md), [Meta](../companies/meta.md), [Amazon](../companies/amazon.md), [Microsoft](../companies/microsoft.md), or [Stripe](../companies/stripe.md).
