# Cold email to recruiters and managers

For students and engineers with 0 to 3 years of experience. You leave with a target list, seven templates, a send schedule, and a pre-send checklist.

> **Tip:** A cold email sends people to your resume and LinkedIn. Finish your [resume checklist](../resume/checklist.md) and [LinkedIn profile](../linkedin/index.md) first, and check [visa sponsorship](../jobs/international-students.md) before you spend an email on a company.

## Your first week

1. Pick 20 to 30 companies, each with 1 to 3 live job IDs. Apply to the roles first ([application strategy](../jobs/application-strategy.md)).
2. For each company, find one hiring manager or engineer on the exact team ([finding people](finding-people.md)).
3. Find and verify each address (see [Find and verify the address](#find-and-verify-the-address)).
4. Write one personal line per person: something they built, wrote, or posted.
5. Fill [template 1](#1-hiring-manager-on-the-exact-team) or [template 3](#3-a-15-minute-chat-with-an-engineer-or-alum-jugals-2026-template). Run the [pre-send checklist](#pre-send-checklist).
6. Send 5 this week, scheduled for 9 AM their time. Log each one in your [tracker](follow-up-and-tracking.md#the-tracker-template) with a follow-up date 5 business days out.

## Where cold email fits

I sent around 700 applications for my first US role and got 3 interviews. What fixed it was talking to people at the companies, not a better resume ([7 videos on networking your way to offers](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to)).

The data agrees on who to email and who to skip:

| Channel | What the data says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Referral from someone who knows you | 40% of referred candidates reach an interview, vs 3% of inbound applicants | [Ashby referral report](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals) (May 2025, 38M+ applications) |
| Cold email to a hiring manager | Net positive at FAANG and FAANG-adjacent companies | [interviewing.io, part 1](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-to-get-in-the-door-at-top-companies-part-1) |
| Cold email to a recruiter, before you apply | Net negative at FAANG unless you already fit what they source for | Same survey |
| Cold referral ask to a stranger | Net negative at FAANG and small startups | Same survey |

The interviewing.io sample skews experienced (average about 8 years), and the post says recruiters are not incentivized to help candidates with under 4 years of experience. For students, recruiter email works in one case: after you apply, with the job ID, to a university recruiter.

### Who to email, in order

| Person | What they control | What you ask for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hiring manager (EM, SDM) on the exact team | Headcount and who interviews | A 15-minute chat about the team | interviewing.io: prefer managers in the role under 2 years, and use email, not LinkedIn |
| Engineer or alum on the team | Can submit a referral | Advice first, referral after a real conversation | See [referrals](referrals.md) |
| University or early-career recruiter | Intern and new grad pipelines | Confirm your application is seen, ask the timeline | Only after you apply. Put the job ID in the subject |
| Professor, lab manager, grad student | Research internships, TA roles, intros | A short meeting or a TA slot | Grad students and lab managers often do the shortlisting ([The $0 Masters in the US](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-0-master-in-the-us-just-one-email)) |
| Founder or CTO at a seed or Series A startup | The whole hiring decision | A call about the role | Startups read applications if you are early ([How I Got My First Startup Offer in 17 Days](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-i-got-my-first-startup-offer)) |

Find names, titles, and profiles for each row with [finding people](finding-people.md).

## The rules, with numbers

| Rule | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Length of the body | 50 to 125 words | [Boomerang, 40M emails](https://blog.boomerangapp.com/2016/02/7-tips-for-getting-more-responses-to-your-emails-with-data/) |
| Subject length | 3 to 4 words did best; stay under 7 | Boomerang; Jugal's Referral Engine prompt caps subjects at 7 words |
| Questions | 1 to 3. Zero questions gets fewer replies | Boomerang |
| Reading level | Plain words. Third-grade level got a 36% lift over college level | Boomerang |
| Personal line | One specific thing they built, wrote, or posted | [Jugal, networking post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to) |
| Template reuse | Change at least one line so it is about them | Jugal, same post |
| Links | 1 to 2 (LinkedIn, GitHub or site). No attachment on a first email to a manager | [interviewing.io, part 2](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-to-get-in-the-door-at-top-companies-cold-out-reach-to-hiring-managers-part-2) |
| The ask | A 15-minute call "this week or next", with two time windows | interviewing.io, part 2 |
| People per company per wave | 1 to 2 (+46% reply rate vs more) | [Hunter, 31M emails](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email) |
| Touches per person | 3 total (first email + 2 follow-ups) | Hunter; Jugal ("twice max") |
| Daily volume | 10 to 15 personal emails in a 10-minute block | [The Job Hunt I Didn't Burn Out Doing](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-i-didnt-burn-out-doing) |

> **Watch out:** In Hunter's 2026 report, 69% of decision makers said it bothers them if AI was used to write an email. Hand edits lifted replies by 18%. Use AI for a draft, then rewrite the personal line yourself.

## Subject lines that work

The subject tells the reader why you wrote. Use 3 to 7 words, and make sure the email delivers what the subject says.

| Weak | Strong | Why the strong one works |
|---|---|---|
| Hi | Quick question about Payments at Stripe | Names the team; a small ask |
| Job opportunity | Summer 2027 SWE Intern applicant, Job ID 12345 | Recruiter can search the ATS by ID |
| Looking for a referral!! | Your post on Kafka retries | Common ground first |
| Hello from a student | Question from a UIUC student | Pattern from [MIT's cold email guide](https://iso.mit.edu/americanisms/cold-emailing/) |
| Following up on my previous email regarding the position | Referred by Priya Shah: Backend role | MIT's guide lists "Referred to you by [name]". Use it only when that person agreed |

Copy-paste subject patterns:

```text
Quick question about [team] at [Company]
Question from a [School] student
[School] [major] student, question about [team]
Referred by [Full Name]: [role] at [Company]
[Season Year] [Role] applicant, Job ID [ID]
Your [talk / post / PR] on [topic]
Question on [team]'s [system or feature]
OA completed, [role] (Job ID [ID])
```

Never use: ALL CAPS, "URGENT", a fake "Re:" or "Fwd:", emojis, or a subject that promises something the email does not contain. CAN-SPAM bans deceptive subject lines (see [email law](#email-law-can-spam-uk-and-eu)).

## Anatomy of a reply-worthy email

Five parts, about 80 to 110 words.

| Part | Job | Words | Example line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Common ground | Prove this is not a mass email | 15 to 25 | "Your post on idempotency keys fixed a bug in my own project." |
| 2. Who you are | One credential | 10 to 15 | "I'm a UIUC CS junior graduating May 2028." |
| 3. Proof | One or two results with numbers | 20 to 35 | "Adding retries with idempotency cut duplicate orders from 14 a week to zero for 2,300 users." |
| 4. The ask | One small, specific request with a time window | 15 to 25 | "Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week or next? I'm free Tue or Thu, 2 to 5 PM PT." |
| 5. Sign-off | Name plus 1 to 2 links | 5 to 10 | "Thanks, Arjun / LinkedIn / GitHub" |

Write part 3 with the XYZ formula from [writing bullets](../resume/writing-bullets.md). If you have no work experience, a small project is proof. When I hunted for my first internship, one thing that worked was cloning a startup's landing page and sending it with my application ([How I Landed My Internship](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-i-landed-my-internship-before)).

### Weak vs strong, same student

Weak (about 130 words, no common ground, no number, a vague ask):

```text
Subject: Job opportunity

Dear Sir/Madam,

I hope this email finds you well. My name is Arjun and I am a third year
Computer Science student at the University of Illinois. I am very passionate
about software engineering and have always admired your company. I have
experience in Java, Python, React, Node, SQL, AWS and many other technologies.
I am a hard worker and a quick learner who works well in teams.

I am writing to ask if there are any internship opportunities available at
your company. I would be very grateful if you could refer me or forward my
resume to the relevant team. I have attached my resume and transcript.

Please let me know if you need anything else. Looking forward to hearing
from you soon.

Thanks and regards,
Arjun
```

Strong (about 85 words, names and details invented for the example):

```text
Subject: Your post on idempotency keys

Hi Priya,

Your post on idempotency keys fixed a bug in my own project. My campus
food-ordering app double-charged on retries; adding idempotency keys cut
duplicate orders from 14 a week to zero for 2,300 users.

I'm a UIUC CS junior and applied to the Summer 2027 SWE Intern role
(Job ID 12345) on your team. Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week
or next? I'm free Tue or Thu, 2 to 5 PM PT.

Thanks,
Arjun
linkedin.com/in/[handle] | github.com/[handle]
```

## Templates

Full library, including LinkedIn notes and recruiter replies: [templates](templates.md). These seven cover cold email.

### 1. Hiring manager on the exact team

Use when the manager has a talk, post, repo, or launch you can name. Based on interviewing.io's advice: no attachment, 1 to 2 links, ask for a chat this week or next.

```text
Subject: Question on [team]'s [system or feature]

Hi [First name],

I [watched your talk / read your post] on [topic]. The part about [detail]
matched what I hit building [project], where [what you did] cut [metric]
by [number].

I applied to [role] ([Job ID]) on your team. Would you be open to a
15-minute call this week or next? I'm free [two windows, their time zone].

Thanks,
[Name]
[LinkedIn] | [GitHub or site]
```

### 2. University or early-career recruiter, after you apply

About 90 words. Send the same day you apply.

```text
Subject: [Summer 2027 SWE Intern] applicant, Job ID [ID]

Hi [Name],

I'm [Full Name], a [year] in [major] at [School], graduating [Month Year].
I applied to [role] (Job ID [ID]) on [date].

Two things that match the role:
- [Project or internship]: [result with a number]
- [Skill from the job description]: [where you used it, with a number]

Is there anything else you need from me to be considered this cycle?

Thanks,
[Name]
[LinkedIn] | [Resume link] | [Work authorization line, if relevant]
```

### 3. A 15-minute chat with an engineer or alum (Jugal's 2026 template)

Verbatim from [7 videos on networking your way to offers](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to).

```text
Subject: Quick question about [team] at [Company]
Hi [Name],
I'm [Name], [one line credential]. I read your [specific post, project, or talk] and [one line on what stuck with you].
I'm applying to [role] at [Company] and would love 15 minutes to hear what the team actually looks for. Happy to work around your calendar.
Thanks either way, [Name]
```

### 4. Interest plus a referral ask (Jugal's 2025 template)

From [The Job Hunt I Didn't Burn Out Doing](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-i-didnt-burn-out-doing), with one change: the original opens with "Hope you're doing well." Here that line is replaced by a common-ground line, because Jugal's 2026 post says generic openers are where most cold emails die.

```text
Subject: Interest in [Job Position] at [Company Name]
Hi [Name],
[Common-ground line: one thing they built, wrote, or posted.] I'm a [Your Role], and I'm interested in the [Job Position] at [Company].
I've worked on [One-liner about your best project/impact].
My background in [Relevant Skills] aligns with [Company Initiative or Product].
Would love to connect or be considered for a referral.
Here's the role: [Insert link]
Resume attached.
Thanks,
[Your Name]
```

Use this one for people you share real common ground with. Cold referral asks to strangers are low-yield; read [referrals](referrals.md) first. Sending it to a hiring manager? Replace "Resume attached." with a resume link, per interviewing.io's no-attachment rule.

### 5. Professor or lab (research internship or TA role)

Jugal's TA email from [The $0 Masters in the US](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-0-master-in-the-us-just-one-email). Follow up after 7 to 10 days.

```text
Subject: TA Opportunity for [Course Name]: [Semester]

Dear Professor [Last Name],
I'm [Your Name], a [Year, Major] student, and I wanted to express interest in a TA position for [Course Name] this upcoming [Fall/Spring]. I earned an A in the course and have experience tutoring [Subject/Topic].
I'd be grateful for any opportunity to support your course and contribute meaningfully. Please let me know if there's a formal process I should follow.
Thank you for your time and consideration.
Best,
[Your Name]
[University, Contact Info]
```

### 6. Quant or HFT firm (guidance ask)

Transcribed from the image in [How to break into $300K+ HFT roles](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-to-break-into-300k-hft-roles). Quant intern applications open early, so send this in summer. See [Jane Street](../companies/jane-street.md), [Citadel](../companies/citadel.md), [Hudson River Trading](../companies/hudson-river-trading.md).

```text
Hi [Name],

I'm a [Year] CS/Math student at [University], and I'm fascinated by the intersection of low-latency systems and quantitative research. I'm preparing for roles in firms like [Company] and would be grateful for any guidance or a quick 15-minute chat on how to best position myself.

Thanks in advance,
[Your Name]
```

### 7. Someone who posted "we're hiring"

```text
Subject: Your post about [team] hiring

Hi [Name],

Saw your post that [team] is hiring a [role]. I [one result with a number,
tied to the role's stack], and I applied today ([Job ID]).

Happy to send a 3-line summary if it helps. Is there anything the team
weighs that the job post does not say?

Thanks,
[Name]
[LinkedIn] | [GitHub]
```

Jugal's shorter version for team leads who posted in the last 3 days: "Hey, I saw your recent post. Curious if your team is hiring for XYZ?" ([source](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-i-didnt-burn-out-doing)).

## Find and verify the address

Full method on [finding people](finding-people.md). The short version:

1. Get the person's full name and the company's email domain (careers page footer or press page).
2. Search [Hunter Email Finder](https://hunter.io/email-finder) with "First Last" plus the domain. Free plan: 50 credits a month, and failed searches are free ([pricing](https://hunter.io/pricing)).
3. If nothing comes back, guess three patterns: `first@`, `first.last@`, `flast@` (from interviewing.io part 2). Large companies often use usernames, so guesses fail more there.
4. Verify every address in [Hunter Email Verifier](https://hunter.io/email-verifier) (free up to 100 a month). Send to Valid, drop Invalid, and run Accept-all results through [Truelist](https://truelist.io/) (free) first.
5. Never send to unverified guesses in bulk. Bounces damage your sender reputation, and Hunter's 2026 average bounce rate was already 3.6%.

## When to send

| Situation | When | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A new intern or new grad posting | Within 1 to 3 days of it going live. Review is rolling, so the posted deadline is almost never the real one | [494 Summer 2027 Internships](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/494-summer-2027-internships-are-already) |
| You just applied | Same day or next morning, with the job ID | Practice norm |
| You met someone at a fair | Within 24 hours | [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-prep-your-elevator-pitch-in-a-flash/) |
| Day of week | Barely matters: Wed and Thu 0.48% reply rate, Mon and Tue 0.45%, Fri 0.44% | [Belkins, 7.5M B2B emails](https://belkins.io/blog/best-time-to-send-email) |
| Time of day | 8 AM to 12 PM in the recipient's time zone had the highest reply rate | Belkins |

Write at night and use [Gmail Schedule send](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/9214606) to deliver at 9 AM their time. Gmail holds up to 100 scheduled emails.

Decision makers in Hunter's 2025 survey said the send day and time do not matter to them ([State of Cold Email 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)). Do not wait for a perfect Tuesday. For the recruiting calendar itself, see [internships](../internships/index.md).

## How many to send

| Mode | Volume | What it gets you |
|---|---|---|
| Light | 5 new people a week | "Five a week for a month is 20 conversations started" ([Jugal](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to)) |
| Standard | 10 to 15 a day in a 10-minute daily block | Jugal's pace in [The Job Hunt I Didn't Burn Out Doing](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-i-didnt-burn-out-doing) |
| Ceiling for a personal Gmail | Stay under 50 a day | Keeps you far from limits and spam complaints |
| Hard limit (do not approach) | 500 a day on personal Gmail; 2,000 on Google Workspace (500 on trial accounts) | [Gmail limits](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/22839), [Workspace limits](https://support.google.com/a/answer/166852) |

Hunter's sweet spot of 20 to 49 sends a day per account comes from sales mailboxes. You are one person writing to individuals, so stay lower and personal.

Budget your free email-finder credits: Hunter's 50 credits a month covers about 50 people. Spend them on hiring managers and team engineers, not on whole departments.

## Deliverability checklist

- [ ] Send from your personal Gmail or your university address. Do not buy a new domain for outreach: it has no sending history and you must set up its SPF or DKIM yourself, which Gmail requires of every sender ([Gmail sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126)).
- [ ] Verify every address before sending (see above).
- [ ] Plain text, no images.
- [ ] Open tracking off. In Hunter's 2026 data, emails without open tracking got 7.4% replies vs 4.4% with it ([Hunter 2026](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email)).
- [ ] At most 2 links. No link shorteners.
- [ ] No attachment on a first email to a manager. Link the resume instead (Google Drive, "Anyone with the link: Viewer", tested in a private window).
- [ ] One personal line per email, written by you.
- [ ] Test your template at [mail-tester.com](https://www.mail-tester.com/) before any mail merge. It gives a spam score for your draft.
- [ ] Remove anyone who says no or asks you to stop, everywhere, for good.
- [ ] Keep spam complaints near zero. Gmail requires every sender to stay below a 0.3% spam rate.

## Mail merge without looking like spam

Use this only when you send more than 10 a day and every row has a line you wrote yourself.

| Tool | Free limit (as of Oct 2026) | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| [Mailmeteor](https://mailmeteor.com/docs/get-started/email-quota) | 50 emails a day | Gmail merge from Google Sheets; Jugal's suggested tool at 10 to 15 a day |
| [YAMM](https://yamm.com/pricing/) | 20 recipients a day | Fine for 20 a day |
| [Streak](https://www.streak.com/pricing) | 50 merges a day | Works inside Gmail. Leave its open tracking off (see the deliverability checklist) |
| [GMass](https://www.gmass.co/blog/pricing-faq/) (paid) | 7-day trial at 50 a day, then paid | Skip unless you already pay |

Sheet columns for the merge:

```text
FirstName | Email | Company | Role | JobID | Hook | Proof | Status | SentDate
```

1. Fill `Hook` by hand for every row: one sentence about their post, talk, repo, or team. Hunter found two custom fields in the body lifted replies by 56%.
2. Fill `Proof` with your most relevant result for that role.
3. Send 10 to 15 rows a day, not the whole sheet.
4. Mark `Status` and `SentDate` the same day, then follow the cadence in [follow-up and tracking](follow-up-and-tracking.md).

## Email law: CAN-SPAM, UK and EU

This is not legal advice. The US law, CAN-SPAM, covers email whose primary purpose is advertising a commercial product or service. A one-to-one job inquiry does not advertise a product, so it is likely outside the law. Follow its rules anyway; they cost nothing.

| CAN-SPAM rule ([FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)) | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| No false or misleading header information | Send from your real name and real address |
| No deceptive subject lines | The subject describes what is in the email |
| Identify the message as an ad | Not relevant to a job inquiry; never disguise a sales pitch as one |
| Include a valid postal address | Required for commercial email. For a job inquiry, a city in your signature is a fine habit |
| Tell recipients how to opt out, and honor it within 10 business days | Add "If this isn't the right person, no worries, I won't follow up" and stop the same day they ask |

Penalties run up to $53,088 per violating email.

UK readers: the ICO says the PECR rule on direct marketing email does not apply to corporate subscribers, but UK GDPR still applies to a named person's work email. Do not hide your identity, give a way to opt out, and respect an objection ([ICO business-to-business guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)). EU country rules vary; the same habits keep you safe.

> **Watch out:** Scammers pose as recruiters too. A real employer never asks you to pay for a job, buy equipment, or deposit a check and send money back ([FTC job scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams)). Check that the sender's domain matches the company and that the role exists on the official careers site.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Opening with "I hope this email finds you well" and a life story | Open with the common-ground line |
| Listing 12 technologies | One result with a number beats a skills list |
| "Any opportunities at your company?" | Name one role and one job ID |
| Asking a stranger for a referral in the first email | Ask for 15 minutes; ask for the referral after you talk ([referrals](referrals.md)) |
| Emailing 10 people at one company the same day | 1 to 2 per company per wave; add a second person only after 3 business days |
| Attaching resume plus transcript plus cover letter | One link, or one PDF named `Firstname_Lastname_Resume.pdf` when a recruiter asked for it |
| Following up 5 times | 3 touches total, then stop |
| Spending outreach on a company that does not sponsor your visa | Check sponsorship first ([how](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-to-check-if-a-company-sponsors)) |
| Guessing an address and sending without verification | Verify every address |

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] One job ID picked, and I have applied or will apply today.
- [ ] Resume tailored to that job ([tailoring](../resume/tailoring.md)).
- [ ] Address verified.
- [ ] Subject is under 7 words and says what is inside.
- [ ] First line is about them, and it is true.
- [ ] One or two results with numbers.
- [ ] One ask, with a time window.
- [ ] 50 to 125 words. I read it out loud once.
- [ ] 1 to 2 links, tested in a private window.
- [ ] No exclamation marks, no "Dear Sir/Madam", no typos in their name or company.
- [ ] Scheduled for 8 AM to 12 PM in their time zone.
- [ ] Logged in my tracker with a follow-up date ([template](follow-up-and-tracking.md#the-tracker-template)).
