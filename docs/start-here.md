# Start here

For anyone opening this repo for the first time. When you finish this page you will have a track, a timeline, a weekly hour budget, and your first week of tasks on a calendar.

## Step 1: Pick your track

| You are | Track | Open first |
|---|---|---|
| A student who wants a summer internship | Intern | [How internships work](internships/index.md) |
| Graduating in the next 12 months, or graduated with under 1 year of full-time work | New grad | [What jobs to apply for](jobs/index.md) |
| A working engineer with 0 to 3 years of experience | Experienced | [Level mapping](jobs/index.md#level-mapping-across-companies) |
| On F-1 or any visa that needs sponsorship (any track) | Your track, plus visa checks | [International students](jobs/international-students.md) |

## Step 2: Check where you are in the hiring year

Big tech reviews applications on a rolling basis. A good application in week one of an opening competes for more interview slots than the same application in week five ([Jugal's internship post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/494-summer-2027-internships-are-already)).

| Track | When applications open (as of Oct 2026) | What to do this week |
|---|---|---|
| Intern, Summer 2027 | Big tech posts July 2026 to Feb 2027. Peak is Sept to Oct 2026. First-round screens Nov to Dec, finals Jan to Feb, most offers Mar to Apr 2027 ([Simplify timeline](https://simplify.jobs/blog/summer-2027-internship-timeline)) | You are in peak season. Apply this week and run the 8 or 12 week plan in parallel |
| New grad, 2027 start | Main window Aug to Oct 2026. A second wave runs Jan to Mar 2027 ([Simplify 2027 new grad roadmap](https://simplify.jobs/blog/swe-interview-prep-roadmap-2027)) | Apply now. Use the 8 week plan so you are ready for the second wave too |
| Experienced, 0 to 3 years | Year-round | Pick the plan that ends when you want to start interviewing |

> **Watch out:** Do not wait until you feel ready to apply. The median search took 82 days from start to first offer in Q2 2026 ([Huntr Q2 2026](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q2-2026)), and rolling review gives the early slots to early applicants. If an interview lands before you feel ready, switch to the 4-week plan below.

Jugal's 30-day plan runs the same way: applications every morning, prep the rest of the day ([post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/want-a-job-in-the-next-30-days-use)).

## Step 3: Set your weekly hour budget

Use these numbers as your default. Tech Interview Handbook suggests 3 months at 11 hours a week for coding prep ([study plan](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/coding-interview-study-plan/)). Grind 75 defaults to 8 weeks at 8 hours a week ([Grind 75](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75/)).

| Your situation | Hours per week | Coding | Applications and outreach | Resume, behavioral, design |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Student with full classes | 10 to 11 | 6 to 7 | 2 | 2 |
| Student on break or summer | 20 to 25 | 15 (about 3 a day) | 3 to 5 | 3 to 5 |
| Working engineer, full-time job | 8 to 10 | 5 | 2 | 2 to 3 (system design if your level needs it) |
| Anyone with an interview in under 4 weeks | 12 to 15 | 8 to 10 | 1 | 3 to 4 |

### Sample week: student with classes (about 11 hours)

| Day | Time | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Mon to Fri | 75 min | 10 min: re-solve 1 old problem. 50 min: 1 Medium or 2 Easy from this week's pattern. 15 min: log it in your tracking sheet |
| Sat | 2 h | 90 min: send 10 applications (tailor the skills line and top 3 bullets for each). 30 min: send 5 outreach messages |
| Sun | 2.5 h | 90 min: a past contest or a mock. 60 min: write or rehearse 1 behavioral story |

### Sample week: working engineer (about 9 hours)

| Day | Time | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Mon to Thu | 60 min | 1 Medium on a 30 minute timer, then read the best solution and log it |
| Fri | Off | Rest. Consistency beats one long weekend |
| Sat | 3 h | 2 timed problems, then 1 hour of system design (if your target level gets a design round) |
| Sun | 2 h | 1 h: applications and outreach. 1 h: behavioral stories from your real projects |

Jugal's own daily routine during his job hunt was "3 LeetCode problems a day (focus on patterns, not volume)", 1 AI mock interview, and 1 peer mock on weekends ([post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-hunt-i-didnt-burn-out-doing)).

## Step 4: Follow the order of operations

Do these in order. Steps 5 to 7 run every week once they start.

1. **Pick 2 role families and your level.** Use [What jobs to apply for](jobs/index.md) and fill in its profile block.
2. **Check visa rules if they apply to you.** Read [International students](jobs/international-students.md). Check sponsorship before you tailor anything ([Jugal's H-1B check](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-to-check-if-a-company-sponsors)).
3. **Build one base resume.** Follow [Resume rules](resume/index.md), [Writing bullets](resume/writing-bullets.md), and [Templates](resume/templates.md). Run the [Final checklist](resume/checklist.md).
4. **Fix your LinkedIn profile.** Work through [Optimize your profile](linkedin/index.md) in one sitting.
5. **Start applying in week 1 or 2.** Use [Where to find jobs](jobs/where-to-find-jobs.md) and [Application strategy](jobs/application-strategy.md). Tailor each one with [Resume tailoring](resume/tailoring.md).
6. **Start coding prep the same week.** Read [How to learn DSA](coding/index.md), then pick one list on [Problem lists](coding/problem-lists.md) and follow [How to practice](coding/how-to-practice.md).
7. **Send 5 outreach messages a week.** Use [Outreach strategy](outreach/index.md), [Message templates](outreach/templates.md), and [Referrals](outreach/referrals.md). "Five a week for a month is 20 conversations started" ([post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to)).
8. **Prepare for online assessments before the first invite.** Read [How OAs work](online-assessments/index.md) and [OA formats by company](online-assessments/company-oa-formats.md).
9. **Write your story bank by the midpoint of your plan.** Use [Story bank](behavioral/story-bank.md), then the page for your target company under [Behavioral](behavioral/index.md).
10. **Add system design only if your level needs it.** Check [Who needs system design](system-design/index.md). Google L3 and Meta E3 loops have no design round ([Hello Interview Meta E3](https://www.hellointerview.com/guides/meta/e3)). Amazon SDE I usually includes an object-oriented design round ([Hello Interview Amazon L4](https://www.hellointerview.com/guides/amazon/l4)).
11. **Do company prep 2 to 3 weeks before each interview.** Use your company's page from [Companies](companies/index.md).
12. **Negotiate every offer.** Follow [The negotiation process](negotiation/index.md) and use [Scripts and emails](negotiation/scripts.md).

## Step 5: Pick a timeline plan

Each plan lists the focus, the pages to use, and what you should have at the end of the week. If you fall behind, repeat a week instead of skipping it.

## How to use the interactive roadmap

1. Open the [interactive roadmap](roadmap.md).
2. Choose your track: Intern, New grad, or Experienced.
3. Work through the phases in order. Each task links to the page or resource you need.
4. Tick each task when it is done. Progress saves in this browser.
5. Use the same browser on the same device. A private window, a different device, or clearing site data starts you from zero.

Every `- [ ]` checklist on the site works the same way: click to tick, and it stays ticked in this browser.

## How to use company pages

1. Open [Companies](companies/index.md) and pick 3 to 5 targets. Companies without a full guide have a question list in [More companies](companies/more/index.md).
2. Read the process and online assessment sections on the day you apply, so the OA does not surprise you.
3. Finish a core list from [Problem lists](coding/problem-lists.md) first. Company lists are the last part of prep, not the first.
4. Start the company list 2 to 3 weeks before the interview. Solve the most frequent problems top to bottom, then the signature problems.
5. Treat frequency as a signal, not a promise. The counts come from user-reported LeetCode company tags in two open datasets: [liquidslr/leetcode-company-wise-problems](https://github.com/liquidslr/leetcode-company-wise-problems) and [snehasishroy/leetcode-companywise-interview-questions](https://github.com/snehasishroy/leetcode-companywise-interview-questions).
6. Follow the 4-week plan at the bottom of each full guide.
7. Read the reported questions and the company's [behavioral page](behavioral/index.md) in the final week.
