# How recruiters find you on LinkedIn

For anyone with a finished profile ([Step-by-step profile](index.md)) who wants recruiters to message first. You finish knowing each filter recruiters use, the profile field it reads, and how to test that you show up.

I set a goal of 30+ recruiter messages in 30 days without changing my actual experience or skills. The result was 47 recruiter messages, a 340% increase in profile views, and 3 interviews at companies I wanted ([How I got 47 recruiter messages within a month](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-i-got-47-recruiter-messages-within)). Below is the search side of that system.

## The tools recruiters search with

| Tool | Who uses it | Reach | What matters for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn Recruiter | Corporate talent teams, including big tech | Full network, "40+ advanced search filters", 150 InMails a month, Open to Work spotlight, AI search | Every filter below applies |
| Recruiter Lite | Often smaller teams and founders | Search "up to your 3rd degree connections", "20+ filters", 30 InMails a month | Only finds you if you are within 3 degrees of them. Your network size matters |
| Sales Navigator | Built for sales; some recruiters and founders use it for sourcing | Boolean in title, company, and keyword fields, about 15 operators max | Jugal's [Profile Playbook](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-linkedin-profile-playbook-how) frames search around it. Same keyword rules |
| Hiring Assistant | Paid AI add-on to Recruiter, rolling out gradually | Searches, sources, sends outreach, and screens from a hiring goal written in plain language | Writes to you with a clearly labeled message. Reply like you would to a person |

Sources: [Recruiter Lite vs Recruiter](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/product-comparison/recruiterlite-vs-recruiter), [Sales Navigator Boolean](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a168061), [Hiring Assistant](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a7104929), [How LinkedIn uses AI agents](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7437598). Figures as of Oct 2026.

## A recruiter search, step by step

A typical search for an entry-level backend role, built from Recruiter's documented filters. Your profile has to survive each step.

1. Types job titles with Boolean: `("software engineer" OR "software developer" OR SDE) NOT (senior OR staff)`.
2. Adds Location (a metro, a country, or people "interested in relocating" to it).
3. Adds Skills: Java, Python, AWS.
4. Narrows by Year of graduation (2026 or 2027), Degrees, and Fields of study.
5. Caps Years of experience at 0 to 2.
6. Clicks spotlights: Open to Work, Interested in your company, Have company connections.
7. Ticks "Hide previously viewed" so only new people show.
8. Reads cards (photo, headline, location, current role) and opens only the ones that match at a glance. A search shows at most 1,000 candidates, 40 pages ([filter definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428)).
9. Sends InMail to the ones whose headline and top Experience match.

With Recruiter's AI search the recruiter can skip steps 1 to 5. They type a plain sentence ("Find a new grad backend engineer in Seattle who knows Java and AWS") or paste the job description, and each result card shows an AI summary of why the person fits ([Recruiter AI Search](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a6509735)). Clear role words and outcomes in your Experience text give that summary something to quote.

## Every filter and the field it reads

Each filter can be set to "Can have", "Must have", or "Doesn't have" ([Filter search results](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414281)). Definitions below are from [Recruiter search filters and definitions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428).

| Filter | What it reads from your profile | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Job titles | Experience titles, current or past | Use standard titles: "Software Engineer Intern", "SDE I". Put target titles in Open to Work too |
| Locations | Profile location; also Open to Work members "interested in relocating" there | Set location to where you want to work. List every acceptable city in Open to Work |
| Workplace types | Only returns Open to Work members | Fill on-site, hybrid, remote in Open to Work |
| Skills | Skills section plus skill keywords in sections where skills are likely listed | Put each target skill in Skills and in at least one Experience bullet |
| Keywords | Text across your profile; ignores stop words (and, or, the, of, at, by, to, for, with, in) | Write exact terms: "distributed systems", not "large-scale backend stuff" |
| Companies | Current or past employers | Link roles to the company's LinkedIn page (pick it from the dropdown) |
| Schools | Education entries | Link your school's LinkedIn page, not free text |
| Year of graduation | End date of each school in Education | Enter your expected end date |
| Degrees, Fields of study | Separate Education fields | Fill both: "Master of Science" and "Computer Science" |
| Years of experience | Start date of your earliest Experience position | Remove unrelated early jobs from Experience |
| Seniority | Derived from your current job titles | Keep "Intern", "New Grad", "I" in titles where true |
| Employment type | Full-time, part-time, contract, internship | Set it on every role |
| Industries | The industry you picked in Edit intro | Software Development, not Higher Education |
| Company followers | Whether you follow the recruiter's company page | Follow all target companies. LinkedIn tells recruiters followers are twice as likely to respond to InMail |
| Spoken languages, Profile languages | Languages section, profile language | Add languages with proficiency (useful for India and Europe roles) |
| Open to work | Your Open to Work setting (Recruiter only) | Turn on Recruiters only |
| Recently joined LinkedIn | Account age, 1 day to 3 months | Nothing to do |

> **Watch out:** Recruiter's skill search also reads your saved resumes if you turn on "Share resume data with recruiters", and it infers skills (Tableau or Power BI imply Data Visualization) ([Recruiter skills help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a593591)). Applicant Skills Match, the alert a job poster gets when an applicant fits, needs you and the job in the same country, except in the EEA. International students applying to US roles: set your US location.

## Spotlights you can trigger

Spotlights are one-click shortcuts at the top of a Recruiter search. Clicking one narrows the results to people in it, so being in one puts you on a much shorter list. Source: [Recruiter spotlights](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414283).

| Spotlight | In Recruiter Lite? | How you get into it |
|---|---|---|
| Open to Work | No (Recruiter only) | Open to Work set to Recruiters only or All members ([Step 15](index.md#step-15-open-to-work-recruiters-only)) |
| Interested in your company | Yes | Click "I'm interested" on the company's page, follow the company page, or like, share, or comment on its posts |
| Have company connections | Yes | Be a 1st-degree connection of anyone who works there |
| Active talent | No | Recently update your profile or share your resume with recruiters. LinkedIn also counts how active you are on the site |
| Verifications | No | Verify your identity or workplace. LinkedIn tells hirers not to treat a missing badge as a negative |
| Applicants | No | Apply to that company's job post |
| Rediscovered candidates | No | That company's team interacted with you before, and you match the new search |

Four free actions put you in four spotlights: Open to Work set to recruiters only (Open to Work), "I'm interested" on target companies (Interested in your company), resume sharing (Active talent), and identity verification (Verifications). Do all four today ([Steps 14 to 16](index.md#step-14-verification)).

## Boolean: what recruiters type

Rules for LinkedIn Recruiter ([Boolean help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415295)):

- Operators are `AND`, `OR`, `NOT` in capital letters, plus `"quotes"` for exact phrases and `( )` for grouping.
- They work in the Job titles, Companies, and Keywords filters.
- `+` and `-` are not supported. Too many facets in one query can break results.
- LinkedIn's own example: `(Job Title: "developer" OR "programmer" OR "engineer") AND Skills: ("java" OR "C++") AND NOT Job Title: ("intern" OR "junior")`.

The free search bar on your own account supports the same operators. Precedence is quotes, parentheses, NOT, AND, OR. No wildcards ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a524335)).

Strings a recruiter might use for early-career roles (illustrative, built from LinkedIn's documented syntax):

```text
Job titles: ("software engineer" OR "software developer" OR SDE OR "backend engineer") NOT (senior OR staff OR principal OR manager)
Keywords:   (Java OR Python OR Go) AND (AWS OR GCP OR Kubernetes) AND ("distributed systems" OR microservices)
Keywords:   ("new grad" OR "university graduate" OR "class of 2027") AND ("computer science" OR CS)
Job titles: (intern OR internship OR co-op) AND (software OR "machine learning")
Keywords:   ("machine learning" OR "deep learning") AND (PyTorch OR TensorFlow) AND (LLM OR RAG)
```

A headline of "Aspiring Tech Enthusiast" and an Experience title of "Developer Ninja" match none of these strings.

## Run the mirror test on yourself

1. Pick the 2 strings above closest to your target. Swap in your stack.
2. Paste each into the LinkedIn search bar on your own account and filter to **People**. Add your city under **Locations**.
3. Open the top 10 profiles. Write down where they put the matching words: headline, title, About, Skills.
4. Compare with your profile. Move missing keywords into the same places, only if you have used them.
5. Repeat in 2 weeks. Free search is personalized by your network, so treat it as a rough check, not the recruiter's exact ranking.

## AI search and Hiring Assistant

LinkedIn's hiring agents read these fields: work experience, location, education, skills, summary, certifications and licenses, volunteering, publications, patents, your Open to Work status and preferences, resumes you made available, and screening answers you made available ([How LinkedIn uses AI agents](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7437598)).

1. Write each Experience bullet so it can be quoted alone: action, system, technology, number.
2. Fill certifications, publications, and volunteering if you have them. The agents read them.
3. Keep Open to Work preferences current. Agents use them.
4. Treat a message labeled as sent by a Hiring Assistant as a real recruiter pipeline. Reply within 2 days.

LinkedIn says Hiring Assistant lets recruiters find candidates "while reviewing less than half the number of profiles" ([LinkedIn Recruiter](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/recruiter)). Recruiters open fewer profiles, so only the top of the ranking gets read.

## Grow the network recruiters can see

Recruiter Lite only searches up to 3rd-degree connections, and the "Have company connections" spotlight needs a 1st-degree link to an employee. Both reward a relevant network.

1. Connect with every classmate, TA, professor, and internship teammate you know. LinkedIn's own rule: "Only send invitations to people you know and trust", and for people you don't know, follow them or connect with a note ([Follow and connect](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a702683)).
2. For each target company, connect with 5 to 10 engineers, ideally alumni from your school. Search: `[Your University] AND ("Software Engineer" OR SDE) AND [Company]`.
3. Connect with university recruiters at your top 20 companies. Search: `("university recruiter" OR "campus recruiter" OR "early career recruiter") AND [Company]`.
4. Spend your 3 free personalized notes a month (200 characters each) on hiring managers or alumni at top targets ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a563153)). Send everyone else a blank request, then message after they accept (messaging 1st-degree connections is free).
5. If someone has Open Profile on (a Premium feature), the Message button works without a connection or InMail credit ([Open Profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a545663)).
6. Send fewer than 100 invitations a week. LinkedIn does not publish the limit; third parties report about 100 a week ([Into the Minds](https://www.intotheminds.com/blog/en/linkedin-100-invitations-week-limit/)).
7. Withdraw invites pending over 3 weeks. Ignored and spam-marked invites trigger restrictions that LinkedIn "won't be able to remove" on request ([invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012/types-of-restrictions-for-sending-invitations)).
8. Optional: during a 2-week outreach sprint, start a free trial of [Sales Navigator](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator) and filter for the exact recruiter or hiring manager on a team, as in [The job-search tool stack I'd actually use in 2026](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/the-job-search-tool-stack-id-actually). Cancel before it bills.
9. Never use auto-invite or auto-message extensions. The [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) bans them, and repeated automation can lead to a permanent restriction ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012/types-of-restrictions-for-sending-invitations)).

Target: 300 to 500 relevant connections before peak recruiting season, and 5 to 10 quality new connections a week, the weekly number in Jugal's [LinkedIn Step By Step guide](https://jugaldb.notion.site/LinkedIn-Step-By-Step-by-Jugal-Bhatt-265af2117b838071a066e4db145acf57). Connection note templates are in [Message templates](../outreach/templates.md). Finding the right people is covered in [Finding people and emails](../outreach/finding-people.md).

```text
Hi [Name], I'm a [CS junior at School] targeting [SWE internships]. Your move from [A] to [B] at [Company] is exactly the path I'm trying to take. Would love to connect.
```

That note is from [7 videos on networking your way to offers](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/7-videos-on-networking-your-way-to). Count characters after you fill it; free notes stop at 200.

## Measure: search appearances

1. Open **Me > View profile**, scroll to **Analytics**, and click **Search appearances** ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a553050)).
2. Read three lists: "Top companies your searchers worked at", "Top job titles of your searchers", and "Job titles you were found for".
3. If "Job titles you were found for" does not include your target title, rewrite the headline and the top Experience title.
4. Change one thing per week, then log the numbers every Monday.

```text
Week of [date]
Search appearances: [n]   (last week: [n])
Profile views: [n]
Recruiter messages: [n]
Job titles I was found for: [list]
Top searcher companies: [list]
Change I made this week: [e.g. moved "Backend Engineer" to the front of headline]
```

## When a recruiter messages you

1. Reply within 48 hours, even if the answer is no. If you stop answering recruiter InMails while Open to Work is on, LinkedIn asks you to confirm and can switch the setting off ([Open to Work help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508)).
2. Check the sender before sharing anything: real photo, verification badge, detailed history, a Company Page with active employees, a company-domain email ([job scam guide](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a9551007)).
3. Never send SSN or national ID, bank details, passport copies, or any payment before a real offer. Treat requests to move to WhatsApp or Telegram immediately as a red flag.
4. Cross-check the role on the company's own careers page.
5. Move real conversations to a call within a week. Prepare with [Negotiation](../negotiation/index.md) before any number comes up.

```text
Interested:
Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out. I'm interested in [role]. I'm a [new grad / MS student] graduating [Month Year], working mostly in [stack]. Here's my resume: [link]. Are you free for a 15-minute call [two time windows, with time zone]?

Not now, keep the door open:
Hi [Name], thanks for thinking of me. I'm not looking right now, but I'd like to stay in touch for [timeframe]. Mind if I reach out then?

Wrong fit, ask for the right one:
Hi [Name], thanks. This role is [senior / outside my area], but I'm targeting [new grad backend roles]. If your team has openings like that, I'd love to hear about them.
```
