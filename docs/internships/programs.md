Steps to land a campus research position:

1. List 10 professors in your department whose recent papers (last 2 years) you can read and roughly understand.
2. Read one paper per professor. Write one specific question about it.
3. Check each lab page for "prospective students" instructions. Follow them exactly.
4. Send the email below to 5 professors. Follow up once after 7 to 10 days.
5. If a professor says yes, ask for a concrete first task you can finish in 2 weeks.

```text
Subject: [Year] [Major] student interested in [specific topic] research in your lab

Dear Professor [Last name],

I'm a [year] [major] student at [School]. I read your paper "[Title]" and [one sentence on what you understood or found interesting].
I have [one relevant skill or course with a grade or project, e.g., built X in Python, A in Machine Learning].

Do you have room for an undergraduate researcher in [semester or summer]? I can commit [N] hours a week and am happy to start with any task that is useful to the lab.

My resume and transcript are attached.

Thank you,
[Name] | [GitHub] | [LinkedIn]
```

### How to get selected for GSoC (start in January)

1. In January, browse past accepted organizations in the [GSoC archive](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/archive). Shortlist 4 whose code you can build on your laptop. The new list came out on Feb 19 in 2026.
2. Read the [GitHub guide to your first open source contribution](https://github.com/readme/guides/first-oss-contribution). Build each shortlisted project locally.
3. When the accepted list is published, keep the 2 organizations that made it. Join each one's chat and introduce yourself in 3 lines: who you are, what you can do, which project idea interests you.
4. Fix 2 or 3 small issues before the proposal window. Use [Good First Issue](https://goodfirstissue.dev/) or the org's own "good first issue" label.
5. Write your proposal early. Include a week-by-week plan, the issues you already fixed (with links), and what is out of scope.
6. Ask a mentor to review your draft at least a week before the deadline.
7. Submit in the contributor window (mid to late March, if 2027 follows 2026). Not selected? Keep contributing and apply to LFX (summer term listed mid-April) or Outreachy (August).

## Conference scholarships and travel grants

Conferences double as recruiting events, and company travel grants can cover flights and hotels. Jugal did his UIUC master's without paying tuition, funding it through scholarships, assistantships and "a lot of applying to things other people never bothered with" ([conference scholarships post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-students-can-find-and-apply-for)).

1. Read Jugal's [conference scholarships post](https://jugaldb.substack.com/p/how-students-can-find-and-apply-for). It has 30 program links and his 5-step method: check eligibility, collect deadlines in one place, build base material once, tailor each one, submit early and stack awards.
2. Check the [Citadel Conference Travel Grant](https://www.citadel.com/careers/programs-and-events/conference-travel-grant/). It covers a conference pass, airfare, lodging and meals, plus Citadel recruiting events. The NeurIPS 2026 grant deadline is Oct 5, 2026.
3. Check [NeurIPS 2026 Financial Assistance](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/FinancialAssistance) and the [USENIX student grants](https://www.usenix.org/conferences/grants) hub for systems conferences.
4. Run Jugal's mentor prompt (below) once with your background, then tailor per program.
5. At the conference, collect 5 names and send each a thank-you note within 24 hours. Templates on [career fairs](../outreach/career-fairs.md).

```text
You are an expert scholarship application mentor who has helped students win conference scholarships and travel grants.

Interview me step by step before writing anything.

Identify gaps in my profile and suggest realistic improvements.

Then help me prepare:

- Essays
- Personal statement
- Resume
- LinkedIn profile
- Project descriptions
- Achievement summaries
- Networking introduction
- Common application questions

Rewrite everything to sound authentic, specific, and memorable without exaggerating my achievements.

Be brutally honest if any part of my application is weak.
```

## Pick your programs in 20 minutes

1. Write your graduation month and year, citizenship or visa status, and country of enrollment on one line.
2. Go down each table above. Strike every row you are not eligible for.
3. From what is left, pick 3: one program open now, one with a deadline in the next 90 days, and one backup (open source or research).
4. Put all 3 deadlines in your calendar with a reminder 14 days before each.
5. Add the regular SWE intern postings from [finding and applying](finding-and-applying.md). Programs are extra shots, not a replacement.
