## Interview process

### New grad

1. **Apply (referral optional).** Apply on apply.careers.microsoft.com (new portal; the old portal was retired in late 2025 per candidate reports). Official intern page: referrals are not required and all candidates get equal consideration. Postings stay open a minimum of 5 days and accept applications on an ongoing basis until filled.
2. **Online assessment.** HackerRank in most 2025 to 2026 reports (Codility in early 2025 and before). Usually 2 coding questions, one easier and one medium or hard, in 60 to 110 minutes. India campus OAs are proctored with tab-switch and paste detection, some camera-on.
3. **Recruiter screen.** 15 to 30 minute call on background, role interest and logistics (US SDE II report, Mar 2026). Some US candidates get a 60 minute technical phone screen instead: half behavioral, half 1 to 2 easy or medium DSA questions, sometimes verbal only with no IDE (US SDE II report, Jul 2025).
4. **Final loop (virtual).** 3 to 4 interviews of 45 to 60 minutes on Microsoft Teams, often on one day or across two days. Each round usually opens with behavioral questions, then coding in a HackerRank or Codility live editor where code must compile and pass your own test cases. One round may be LLD or design. India loops: DSA, LLD, then an 'AA' (as appropriate) round with a senior manager that is mostly behavioral and project deep dive.
5. **Decision and team placement.** Official: candidates are first assessed for a functional role, then the final round is for a specific role within an organization; Microsoft allows up to 90 days for a decision. Offers sometimes come the same or next day after the loop; other candidates wait 2+ weeks with no update.

### Intern

1. **Apply to a track posting.** Summer 2027 US SWE intern postings are track-based (Fullstack Product, Cloud and Distributed Backend, AI/ML and LLM, Data Platform/Analytics, Security and Identity, CoreAI). Applications accepted during the first week of each month from August through February, or until filled. Explore (second-year) windows: first week of each month October to December. Need at least one semester remaining after the internship.
2. **Online assessment.** HackerRank, 2 questions (basic plus intermediate), about 75 minutes for India on-campus 2027 interns (Aug to Sep 2026 reports). Extern (secondary) reports 60 to 90 minutes, 2 to 3 LeetCode mediums, and that referrals may skip the OA.
3. **Screen round.** Official: virtual, assessed for a functional role (for example software engineer) across all teams hiring that role.
4. **Final round (2 technical interviews).** Usually 2 interviews of 45 to 60 minutes on the same day, each with one coding problem plus fundamentals. India campus reports add OOP pillars, OS (paging, page faults), DBMS modelling and a resume deep dive. Some candidates get a third behavioral round.
5. **Team match and offer.** Final round is for a specific role in an organization; interests you share with recruiters influence placement. Up to 90 days for a decision (official). India campus: interview invites 3 days after the OA; Round 2 link sent 20 minutes after Round 1 (Sep 2026 report).

### With 1 to 3 years of experience

With 1 to 3 years you interview for 60 (India 'SDE 1 L60' loops often require 1 to 2 YOE) or 61/62 (Software Engineer II). Typical 61/62 flow: recruiter call, HackerRank OA with 2 questions (one medium, one hard), 60 minute phone screen, then a loop of 3 to 4: two coding rounds (trees, graphs, DP), an LLD round (message queue with producer-consumer and locking, cache with pluggable eviction, notification module, cart system) and an AA round. US 'hiring events' run one-day loops of 3 coding plus 1 system design (Jan 2026 reports); HLD prompts include Instagram feed. Some loops are batched to the next month ('I will reach back out when April loops open up', Mar 2026).

## Online assessment

- **Platform:** HackerRank (most 2025 to 2026 reports, including 'Microsoft Global Talent Acquisition' invites); Codility in early 2025 and earlier cycles
- **Format:** Usually 2 coding questions in 60 to 110 minutes (India campus intern OA: 75 minutes; L60 India OA: 110 minutes). Feb 2025 Redmond SDE II OA on Codility: 3 questions in 60 minutes. No separate behavioral or work-style section reported.
- **Notes:** Passing all visible tests does not guarantee an interview: an SSE candidate solved both questions twice and was still 'Not Selected' (Feb 2026), and new grad applicants report long silences after the OA. Proctoring rules prohibit copying code, pasting external content and switching tabs.

Prepare with [How to pass an OA](../online-assessments/strategy.md) and compare formats in [OA formats by company](../online-assessments/company-oa-formats.md).

## Coding rounds

- **Rounds:** Interns: 2. New grad and 59/60: 2 to 3 coding rounds inside a 3 to 4 round loop. 61/62: 2 to 3 coding rounds plus LLD or HLD.
- **Style:** LeetCode easy to medium with some hards, often wrapped in practical framing (calendar booking, session overlap, API response parsing, safe path in a string). Expect follow-ups (in-place, MxN, one-pass, allow one blocker). Trees, strings, arrays, linked lists, graphs and sliding window dominate; DP appears occasionally. interviewing.io: 'Tree questions are most popular'.
- **Environment:** Microsoft Teams video plus HackerRank live interview or Codility live editor; several 2025 to 2026 US reports say code had to compile and run against test cases you write. Some screens are verbal with no IDE. Pick a language you know well; interviewing.io notes C#, Java or Python are easiest for interviewers.
- **Graded on:** Correct, clean, bug-free code; testing your own code with edge cases; time and space complexity; implementation speed (an L60 candidate was rejected with feedback to improve implementation speed); communication. Official tips: show your reasoning, ask clarifying questions, state assumptions.
- **Reported focus topics:** Arrays and strings (sliding window, two pointers, one-pass constraints), Trees and BSTs (traversals, insert and delete, construction), Linked lists (copy with random pointer, reversal), Graphs (BFS/DFS, topological sort, grids), Intervals and scheduling (calendar booking, concurrent sessions), Binary search on the answer, LLD in code: LRU cache, notification service, message queue with concurrency, CS fundamentals for India intern loops: OOP, OS paging, DBMS, Microsoft-tagged LeetCode, last 3 months (snehasishroy repo, July 2026 snapshot): Two Sum, Longest Palindromic Substring, Add Two Numbers, Unique Paths, Trapping Rain Water, Subarray Sum Equals K, Sliding Window Maximum, Search in Rotated Sorted Array, Number of Islands, Merge Intervals, LRU Cache

Run every practice problem through [the 45-minute framework](../coding/interview-framework.md) and the [code quality rubric](../coding/code-quality.md).

## Beyond LeetCode

## System design

Interns and fresh 59 hires: usually no HLD, but expect OOP or data-modelling discussion (users and groups data structure, DBMS case study). 60 with 1 to 2 years: an LLD round is common in India loops, sometimes with runnable code (cache with strategy-pattern eviction, notification module, cart backend with REST APIs). 61/62: LLD (message queue with concurrency) and HLD (Instagram feed, autocomplete for millions of users). interviewing.io says Microsoft design discussions lean on compliance, auditing, data pipelines and EU data rules.

## Behavioral

**Framework:** Microsoft culture (growth mindset, customer obsessed, diverse and inclusive, One Microsoft) and the values respect, integrity and accountability. Official interview tips ask for STAR(R): Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflection. Core competencies listed in prep guides: collaboration, drive for results, customer focus, influencing for impact, judgment, adaptability. ([official page](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/culture))

**What they look for:**

- Growth mindset: learning from failure and seeking feedback
- Collaboration and 'One Microsoft' behavior, not blaming teammates
- Customer focus and empathy
- Clear ownership of your individual contribution to projects
- Reflection: what you would do differently (the extra R in STAR(R))
- Prepared, specific questions for the interviewer (official tip: do not say 'nothing')

**Questions to prepare:**

- What was the most challenging part of your project and how did you solve it? (L60 loop, Feb 2026)
- What exactly did you build on this project, and what was the measurable impact?
- Tell me about your hackathon experience (AA round, L60, Nov 2025)
- Tell me about a time you resolved a conflict with a teammate
- Tell me about a project that failed and what you learned
- Give an example of when you showed a growth mindset
- Why Microsoft, and why this team?

Build your answers with the [story bank](../behavioral/story-bank.md). Company detail: [behavioral guide](../behavioral/microsoft.md).

## Tips

- Apply in the first week of the month: Summer 2027 SWE intern tracks accept applications during the first week of each month from August through February, Explore from October to December.
- Pick the 1 or 2 intern tracks that match your projects (Fullstack, Cloud backend, AI/ML and LLM, Data, Security, CoreAI); the CoreAI posting lists experience with AI coding tools such as GitHub Copilot and Claude Code as preferred.
- Write code that compiles and runs in the live editor, and test it with your own cases before the interviewer asks.
- Prepare STAR(R) stories with a Reflection line; Microsoft's own interview tips add the extra R.
- For 60 to 62 loops, practice one LLD in working code within 35 to 45 minutes: cache with eviction strategies, notification service, producer-consumer queue.
- Treat the final AA round as a senior-leader behavioral and project deep dive: know your exact contribution, numbers and trade-offs.
- In the OA, never switch tabs or paste external code; HackerRank flags both and some 2026 OAs ran camera-on.
- Ask the interviewer real questions at the end; the official student guide warns against answering 'nothing'.
