## Interview process

### New grad

1. **Application.** Lyft's early talent page covers interns, new graduates and apprentices, but no US new grad SWE posting was open on Lyft's Greenhouse board on 2026-10-04 (only Summer 2027 internships and experienced roles). Most early-career SWE openings in 2025 to 2026 reports are in Toronto, Montreal and Mexico City.
2. **Recruiter screen (about 30 min).** Background, role fit, work authorization or status in the country, logistics; some recruiters add a few multiple-choice technical questions (Android, 2026).
3. **Technical screen (about 60 min) or Byteboard.** Live LeetCode-style problem on CoderPad, usually medium (sometimes hard: sliding window median, minimum window substring), with follow-ups. Toronto and Mexico City candidates in 2025 report a Byteboard take-home or proctored session first (read a design doc, comment on it, then implement related features).
4. **Onsite (virtual).** Reported rounds for T3/T4: laptop round (about 90 min; practical OOP problem on your own setup, write your own tests, then extend), coding / CS fundamentals (60 min), system design (60 min, product flavored), behavioral (30 to 45 min, often with an engineering manager; includes inclusion questions). Toronto SWE example (Oct 2025): recruiter 30 min, coding screen 60 min, onsite system design 60 min, coding 60 min, behavioral 30 min.
5. **Team matching.** Matching with teams happens after the onsite; candidates report long waits and no offer when no team picks them (Dec 2025).

### Intern

1. **Application.** Summer 2027 postings went live in Sep 2026. Requirement example (Backend, SF): pursuing a BS or MS in CS graduating Dec 2027 to Summer 2028; master's students with prior work need under 2 years full-time experience. SF interns work hybrid, in office Mon, Wed and Thu.
2. **Online assessment or Byteboard.** Reports vary by track: a 2-hour OA (Canada, Nov 2025); a non-proctored multi-file CoderPad assessment with written and coding questions (US, interviewed Nov 2024); a Byteboard interview (read a document and comment, then implement) for Canada and Mexico interns (2025); ML intern OA built around a provided class (Toronto, Dec 2025).
3. **Technical interview (about 60 min).** LeetCode-style on CoderPad, medium (e.g. rotting oranges; binary search through a provided class for ML interns).
4. **Laptop interview(s) (about 90 min).** One Canada SWE intern (Nov 2025) had two 90-min laptop rounds after the technical. Mobile interns build on starter code (Android 2026: finish an icon-matching app; passing needs at least 3 of the steps).
5. **Behavioral (some tracks).** Short behavioral or recruiter conversation; data science interns get a separate stats/product loop.

### With 1 to 3 years of experience

T4 and above: recruiter call, technical phone screen (often a well-known LeetCode medium or hard; staff candidates report a Blind-list hard), then a virtual onsite of about 4 rounds that can be split over days: laptop round (Senior SF 2026: 'basically OOD'; Staff 2026: algorithm plus your own test cases), system design (pagination API, real-time chat with delivery guarantees, 1:1 chat, product voting, donations site, social feed), behavioral/experience (bar-raiser style for staff), sometimes a domain round (security, data modeling for data engineers). A Senior AI Engineer report (Jun 2026) describes 1 coding round followed by 4 loop interviews. Candidates who pass the DSA round but lose the role may be allowed to skip to the final stage within six months (Toronto, 2025).

## Online assessment

- **Platform:** Varies: CoderPad (non-proctored multi-file assessment), Byteboard (doc review + implementation), or a timed OA (90 min or 2 hours) on an unnamed platform.
- **Format:** Examples: 90-min OA with two problems (array transformation, graph traversal) for an SDE in Canada (Jul 2025); 2-hour OA for a Canada SWE intern (Nov 2025); Byteboard with a design doc to annotate then a related implementation (Canada/Mexico interns 2025).
- **Notes:** Byteboard reports mention it is closer to real work than LeetCode; one Toronto mobile candidate said they used ChatGPT during a Byteboard take-home and passed (allowed use is not confirmed). Follow the rules stated in your invite.

Prepare with [How to pass an OA](../online-assessments/strategy.md) and compare formats in [OA formats by company](../online-assessments/company-oa-formats.md).

## Coding rounds

- **Rounds:** Interns: 1 technical (60 min) plus 1 to 2 laptop rounds (90 min) after an OA/Byteboard. T3/T4: 1 phone screen plus laptop round plus sometimes a coding/CS fundamentals round onsite.
- **Style:** LeetCode medium with Lyft framing; frequent patterns are sliding window (minimum window substring, sliding window median), intervals (meeting rooms II, car pooling, merge intervals), BFS/DFS on grids (rotting oranges) and hash maps with state. Laptop round is practical: design classes for a real problem, make it work, add tests, then extend for new requirements.
- **Environment:** CoderPad for screens (Lyft-provided docs and a mock-interview video for mobile tech screens per a 2025 iOS review). Laptop round on your own machine; a third-party guide says internet use is allowed. Frontend screens may use CodePen (build a 7-segment clock in HTML/CSS, 30 min). Mobile rounds use Xcode or Android Studio starter projects.
- **Graded on:** Correctness and tests, clean structure, performance, communication. Aced (formerly Exponent) reports the laptop round weighting as correctness 45%, clean code 35%, performance 20% (not confirmed by Lyft).
- **Reported focus topics:** Sliding window (minimum window substring, sliding window median), Intervals and sweep line (meeting rooms, car pooling, merge intervals), BFS/DFS on grids and graphs, Hash maps with state management, Practical OOP with tests (laptop round), Reading and extending code from a spec (Byteboard), Product system design: chat, voting, feeds, crawlers, pagination APIs, Behavioral: ownership, failure, inclusion

Run every practice problem through [the 45-minute framework](../coding/interview-framework.md) and the [code quality rubric](../coding/code-quality.md).

## Beyond LeetCode

Laptop round (90 min, practical OOP with your own tests, extend for new requirements). Byteboard (annotate a technical design doc, then implement part of it). Mobile build-on-starter-code rounds (Android icon matching, Kotlin minesweeper, iOS Xcode starter project). Frontend CodePen UI tasks (7-segment real-time clock). Domain rounds for security and data engineering (data modeling).

## System design

Expected from T4; Toronto T3/T4-level SWE loops in 2025 to 2026 also included a 60-min system design, so new grads should prepare basics. Flavor is product design with APIs and data models: real-time 1:1 chat, product voting, donations website (shared with DoorDash), social feed generation, Wikipedia crawler (reports include an intern level), pagination API, ride-sharing data models. Laptop rounds double as LLD/OOD.

## Behavioral

**Framework:** Lyft does not publish a values list on its careers pages as of Oct 2026; pages describe the purpose 'to serve and connect' and say culture 'revolves around our core values'. Third-party guides cite older values such as 'Disagree and Commit', 'Dive Deep' and 'All-in ownership' (unverified). Use STAR with metrics. ([official page](https://www.lyft.com/careers/life-at-lyft))

**What they look for:**

- Ownership of a project you are proud of: your role, impact, and how you measured it
- Handling failure and production bugs you caused
- Persuading colleagues and disagreeing with cross-functional partners constructively
- Inclusion and belonging: repeated questions on what inclusion means to you and how you promote it
- Collaboration in a low-ego, cross-functional team (stated in job postings)
- Structured answers; one 2025 report calls the behavioral round 'very strict and structured'

**Questions to prepare:**

- What's inclusion for you? (SWE, Canada, Jul 2026)
- Tell me about a recent project you're proud of. What was your role, impact, and how did you measure it? (SWE, Canada, Jul 2026)
- Tell me about a failure. (SWE, Canada, Jul 2026)
- Did you convince a colleague about your solution, and how? (SWE, Canada, Jul 2026)
- What does diversity and inclusion mean to you? (Hello Interview, mid-level, 2025-08)
- Tell me about a time you promoted inclusion in the workplace. (Hello Interview, 2026-05)
- Tell me about something you are most proud of and why. (Hello Interview, 2026)
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a cross-functional partner. (Hello Interview, 2026)
- Tell me about how you handled a production bug you caused. (Sr SWE, Canada, 2025)

Build your answers with the [story bank](../behavioral/story-bank.md). Company detail: [behavioral guide](../behavioral/other-companies.md).

## Tips

- Rehearse a 90-minute laptop round: build a small class-based solution in your own environment, write tests, then extend it for a new requirement without rewriting.
- If you get a Byteboard invite, practice reading a short design doc, leaving specific review comments, and then implementing a feature from it.
- Prepare an inclusion story; Lyft behavioral rounds repeatedly ask what inclusion means to you and how you promoted it.
- Drill sliding-window and interval problems until you can code them in under 25 minutes; screens have included hard variants (sliding window median, minimum window substring).
- Interns: Summer 2027 postings opened in Sep 2026; apply in the first week and expect hybrid office days (Mon, Wed, Thu in SF).
- US new grad SWE postings were not open in Oct 2026; watch Toronto, Montreal and Mexico City postings and the early talent page.
- Keep other processes alive after a good onsite: team matching decides the offer and can take weeks.
- If you clear the coding round but lose the role, ask the recruiter about skipping to the final round on a new role within six months.
