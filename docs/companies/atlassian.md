## Interview process

### New grad

1. **Application.** Apply via the Interns/Graduates filter on the careers site. Rolling review. Official recruiting windows: US and Canada Sep to Mar (grad start July), India Jul to Dec (grad start July), ANZ Feb to Jun (grad start February).
2. **Assessment (mandatory).** Official early careers guide: SWE candidates take an online coding test and must clear it to interview. Reported formats: HackerRank, about 90 min, 2 to 4 DSA questions (India 2026 grad drive had at least 4, incl. 'Count Bitonic Subsequences'); 2027-grad India OA (Aug 2026) asked a weighted 'make array distinct' problem. UK SWE Graduate (Sep 2026) reported psychometric assessments as round one.
3. **Recruiter / eligibility screen.** About 15 min: eligibility, motivation, logistics (Graduate SWE, Australia, Aug 2025).
4. **Interview loop (3 to 4 virtual interviews).** Official guide: craft interviews (technical) plus leadership and values interviews, STAR format. For SWE grads this is usually 1 to 2 coding interviews of 45 to 60 min (DSA in your own IDE, sometimes a code design problem), a values interview (45 min) and sometimes a manager/leadership interview. A P30 (SWE-1, India, Sep 2026) report shows a two-part DSA problem (longest constant-difference streak, then allow one replacement).
5. **Hiring committee and team match.** A hiring committee reviews all feedback independently and decides (official). Team matching follows; reports of P30/P40 candidates waiting weeks in team match (2025 to 2026).

### Intern

1. **Application.** Penultimate-year Bachelor's or Master's students in CS or related fields; rolling review, apply early. About 300 to 500 interns a year is a third-party figure (unverified).
2. **Online assessment.** HackerRank-style OA. Reports: US 90 min with 2 to 3 LC-medium questions (Fall 2025); Sydney '2 mediums and 1 easy' (2025); India campus OLT 2 to 3 questions around Codeforces 1600 (DP, graphs, bitmasking), with 2+ solved needed to shortlist.
3. **Technical interview (45 to 60 min).** Live coding with one engineer over Zoom: LeetCode easy-medium with emphasis on approach, clarity and communication (rank-teams-by-votes style hash map voting, LCA, LRU cache, intervals). NEW in 2026: several intern loops (Sydney Apr to May 2026; IIT Guwahati and IIT Kharagpur Aug 2026) used a 1-hour AI-assisted coding interview in a HackerRank IDE, e.g. fixing a bug in a small backend web app with an AI agent. Frontend interns build a UI component (e.g. nested bullet list).
4. **Values / behavioral interview.** 30 to 45 min with an engineer or engineering manager, questions mapped to each Atlassian value; India campus loops call this the HR or 'LNV' round. Some loops add a recruiter call before the final round.
5. **Offer.** US intern timeline example: applied Sep 9, OA Sep 20, round 1 Oct 18, round 2 Nov 3, offer call Nov 9 (2025 cycle). Sydney candidates report 3 to 4 months end to end.

### With 1 to 3 years of experience

For 1 to 3 yrs (P30/P40) the loop is staged. Screen: Karat third-party interview (60 min: about 20 min of 5 rapid-fire system design scenarios plus 2 easy-medium DSA problems), reported for P30 and P40 in 2025; an Oct 2026 report describes a technical screen of 20 min coding plus 15 min HLD. Stage 1: 2 coding interviews of 60 min each, Data Structures (own IDE, tests, debugger allowed) and Code Design. Official prep PDFs dated Jul and Aug 2026 say Code Design is now AI-enabled: a HackerRank IDE with AI coding assistants and an existing code repository you extend, fix or refactor, in a language you may not know. Stage 2: 60-min system design (ladder of follow-ups). Stage 3: 60-min management interview (own outcomes, expertise-driven improvements, initiative, team contribution) and 45-min values interview. Each stage is gated by recruiter feedback. Hiring committee then levels you; candidates report being downleveled P50 to P40 for skipping clarifying questions in design.

## Online assessment

- **Platform:** HackerRank (India 2026-grad internship drive screenshot); an Atlassian-branded timed IDE with sections was used for the 2027-grad OA (Aug 2026). UK graduate track started with psychometric tests (Sep 2026).
- **Format:** 2 to 4 coding questions, about 90 min (US intern 2025); India campus OLT 2 to 3 questions of roughly Codeforces 1600 difficulty (DP, graphs, bitmasking, greedy, XOR). Sydney: 2 medium plus 1 easy. Data engineering intern OAs mix SQL and LeetCode.
- **Notes:** OA is mandatory for early careers (official). Reported 2026 OA problems: count bitonic subsequences mod 1e9+7 (HackerRank, Q4 of 4); minimum total cost to make all array values distinct when incrementing A[i] costs C[i]. India campus shortlists are small (one 2024 campus: 15 shortlisted, 5 offers).

Prepare with [How to pass an OA](../online-assessments/strategy.md) and compare formats in [OA formats by company](../online-assessments/company-oa-formats.md).

## Coding rounds

- **Rounds:** Intern and new grad: 1 to 2 coding interviews (45 to 60 min). P30/P40 experienced loop: 2 coding interviews of 60 min (Data Structures + Code Design), after a Karat or technical screen.
- **Style:** LeetCode easy to medium for interns; medium (sometimes hard, e.g. All O`one) for P40, usually wrapped in an Atlassian product story (Jira activity, Confluence audit logs, support agent ratings, voting). Code design problems are multi-part and extensible (Snake game, tennis court booking with maintenance breaks, middleware router with wildcards, rate limiter, customer/product rating system). Some loops ask follow-ups on concurrency.
- **Environment:** Your own IDE in your chosen language, screen-shared over Zoom, with your favorite test framework ready; CodePair offered if you cannot set up an IDE; using a debugger is encouraged (official backend prep PDF). AI-enabled Code Design rounds (2026) run in a HackerRank IDE with AI coding assistants and an existing repo. Frontend: browser coding in your framework (set up a page that prints the current time before the call).
- **Graded on:** Official: coding skills, code quality, conceptual thinking, adaptability, navigating blockers, communication; testing matters (TDD recommended); show alternative solutions and why you chose one. 'Clean code matters, but what's most important is how you think.' AI round: how you plan with AI, articulate prompts, and validate the result (candidate report Sep 2026).
- **Reported focus topics:** Hash maps with custom sorting and tie-breaks (voting, ratings), Extensible OOP / code design with unit tests (Snake, booking, router, rating systems), Trees: LCA in n-ary and org-chart trees, Intervals and sweep line, Design data structures (LRU, All O`one, stock price tracker, top-K), DP, graphs and bitmasking for India campus OAs, Strings and prefix sums, AI-assisted coding in an unfamiliar codebase (2026), System design (P40+): tagging, top-K feeds, crawlers

Run every practice problem through [the 45-minute framework](../coding/interview-framework.md) and the [code quality rubric](../coding/code-quality.md).

## System design

Not a standard round for interns or most new grads; P30 Karat screens include about 20 min of rapid-fire design scenarios (scaling bottlenecks, cost estimation, unreliable third-party API, large file uploads). From P40: one 60-min system design (whiteboard, Zoom whiteboard or drawing tool) on Atlassian-flavored prompts: tagging system across Jira/Confluence/Bitbucket, top-K popular or most-engaged Confluence pages (exclude already-viewed), web crawler, scorecard service, color picker. LLD is covered by the Code Design coding round (Snake, router, booking, ratings).

Start with [who needs system design](../system-design/index.md), then the [framework](../system-design/framework.md).

## Behavioral

**Framework:** Atlassian's five values: Open company, no bullshit; Build with heart and balance; Don't #@!% the customer; Play, as a team; Be the change you seek. Answer in STAR format. ([official page](https://www.atlassian.com/company/values))

**What they look for:**

- Stories mapped to each of the five values (official prep PDF lists prompts per value)
- User-centered decisions and the trade-offs you made for the customer
- Handling difficult conversations and conflict, adapting communication style
- Advocating for change and what you learned from the outcome
- Owning outcomes, using expertise to drive improvements, taking initiative, helping the team succeed (management interview areas)
- Concrete metrics for impact, since behavioral performance feeds leveling
- Values interviewer is usually from a different department than the hiring team, so keep it non-technical unless invited

**Questions to prepare:**

- How have you helped make a team or individual more successful if they weren't performing well?
- Have you ever had to put the team's goals above personal ambitions?
- When have you advocated to push for change? What was the outcome?
- How have you put the customer first? What trade-off did you make?
- What's your approach when having difficult conversations? How have you resolved conflict?
- What are examples when you or the team missed the mark? How did you react and fix it?
- Tell me about a time you advocated for the customer (Data Engineering Intern, 2025)
- How do you use agents to resolve issues in a repository? (SWE Intern, IIT Kharagpur, Aug 2026)
- What was a trade-off you dealt with at your last internship? (SWE Intern, Vancouver, 2026)

Build your answers with the [story bank](../behavioral/story-bank.md). Company detail: [behavioral guide](../behavioral/other-companies.md).

## Tips

- Before any coding round, open a blank project in your strongest language with a test framework (JUnit, pytest) ready; you will write, run and test code on screen share, and a debugger is allowed.
- Drill the multi-part repeats (tennis court booking, Snake, agent ratings, router): finish part 1 fast and keep classes open for parts 2 and 3.
- For 2026 loops, practice extending an unfamiliar repo with an AI assistant and narrate how you check its output; Atlassian says it is fine to say you are new to the language.
- Write 1 to 2 STAR stories per value (5 values) with numbers; the values interviewer is often from another department, so keep stories non-technical unless asked.
- Ask clarifying questions in design rounds even when the answer seems obvious; a 2026 candidate was downleveled for skipping them.
- Apply in your region's window (US Sep to Mar, India Jul to Dec, ANZ Feb to Jun); review is rolling.
- India campus OAs are harder than the interviews (DP, graphs, bitmask around Codeforces 1600); aim to fully solve 2+ problems.
- Keep other processes going until you have a signed offer: team matching stalled for several candidates around the March 2026 layoffs.

## 4-week plan for Atlassian

Do this after you finish a core list like [Grind 75 or NeetCode 150](../coding/problem-lists.md).

- [ ] Week 1: Solve problems 1 to 20 from the most frequent list. Time-box each at 30 minutes.
- [ ] Week 2: Solve problems 21 to 40. Re-solve any you failed in week 1 without looking.
- [ ] Week 3: Solve the signature problems and every reported question above. Do 2 timed mock interviews.
- [ ] Week 4: Write 8 stories for the Atlassian's five values: Open company, no bullshit; Build with heart and balance; Don't #@!% the customer; Play, as a team; Be the change you seek. Answer in STAR format. round. Do 2 full mock loops. Review the online assessment and system design notes above.
