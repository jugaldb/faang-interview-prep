## Interview process

### New grad

1. **Apply to a specific team posting.** There is no central Apple loop: you apply to a team's requisition on jobs.apple.com and the recruiter and interviewers belong to that team (Aced, interviewing.io). Shortlisting is team-specific, so tailor your resume to each posting (Mar 2025 ICT3 report). You can interview with several teams at once (interviewing.io).
2. **Recruiter and/or hiring manager screen.** 15 to 30 min recruiter call (background, why Apple, compensation expectations), sometimes followed by a hiring manager conversation that can include technical questions (Aced).
3. **Online assessment (some teams).** Used by some teams, not all. Reported formats: 2 coding problems in 60 to 70 min on CoderPad or a similar platform (IS&T SWE Hyderabad, May 2025: Java-only, 70 min; India Junior SWE for freshers, Aug 2025: Java-only; Junior SWE ICT2, Aug 2025: Java or Python), sometimes plus 15 to 20 MCQs on the role's stack (Jan 2025 OA: 2 coding + 15 MCQ; IC3 Frontend, Jun 2025: 1 hour, 2 coding + 20 JavaScript/React MCQs).
4. **Technical screen(s).** 1 to 2 interviews of 45 to 60 min, usually on CoderPad: DSA plus CS fundamentals or language depth. A US IS&T Early Career candidate (Aug 2026) was told by the recruiter to expect 'a quick intro, CS fundamentals, and around 15 minutes of coding'. A Java backend role for 0 to 3 years (Feb 2026) scheduled a 60-min CoderPad round on Core Java, data structures and problem solving. India IS&T rounds mix DSA with Java, Spring Boot, OOP and microservices theory.
5. **Onsite loop.** Virtual or in person, about 3 to 8 rounds depending on team (Aced blog: 3 to 8 rounds of 45 to 60 min; interviewing.io: 6 to 8; Aced guide: 4 to 7 rounds counting screens): coding, domain (iOS, frontend, backend, systems), sometimes LLD or design, and a hiring manager or behavioral round centered on 'Why Apple?'. India Junior SWE for freshers (Aug 2025): DSA round, Java/Python/microservices/LLD round, techno-managerial round.
6. **Debrief and offer.** Teams decide in live debriefs; the hiring manager has the most influence (interviewing.io). Offers then need approvals, which can take weeks: an ICT3 candidate (Mar 2025) wrote that Apple 'requires 4 levels of signatures up to the VP level to roll out the offer'; a Jun 2025 candidate had a verbal offer with the written offer 'pending business approvals' for over 3 weeks. Some candidates are told after the loop that the position was filled by someone else (IC3 Frontend, Jun 2025).

### Intern

1. **Apply.** Search 'Students: Internships' on jobs.apple.com; postings are team-specific and appear through the year (Summer 2027 GPU intern postings appeared Aug and Sep 2026 per prior verification).
2. **Interviews (US).** Usually 1 to 2 team interviews of about 45 min (an Apple Vision Pro intern candidate in Mar 2025 was scheduled for one 45-min interview); expect coding plus project discussion, but US intern reports are sparse.
3. **India IS&T intern (Oct 2025 report).** Round 1 technical elimination (about 50 min): project deep dive, CoderPad DSA (rotate matrix, merge k sorted lists, longest mountain, LRU cache design), complexity theory and networking (DNS, TCP vs UDP, caching, L1/L2/L3 cache). Shortlist email within 30 min. Round 2 managerial and HR (about 50 min): 5 to 6 behavioral questions each on Collaboration, Invention and Results. Offer reported the next day.

### With 1 to 3 years of experience

With 1 to 3 years (ICT3 range), expect a recruiter or HM screen, 1 to 2 technical screens, then 3 to 6 onsite rounds: coding, language internals (Java concurrency, Spring Boot, React hooks), LLD, and a team-specific system design (BookMyShow, TinyURL, distributed rate limiter; a Senior Systems Engineer reported fraud detection with rate limiters). Questions about how you use GenAI tools appear in 2025 to 2026 screens (Hyderabad Ads Delivery, Nov 2025; Hyderabad SWE offer, May 2026). One Sep 2026 report (9 YOE Java role) lists an 'AI assisted coding round' in the loop; Aced (Oct 2026) says Apple 'hasn't announced or adopted the AI-assisted coding rounds that Meta and Google have started piloting', and interviewing.io says AI use is prohibited, so treat AI rounds as team experiments and ask your recruiter.

## Online assessment

- **Platform:** Varies by team: CoderPad link or HackerRank-style assessment; many US teams skip the OA and start with a recruiter or hiring manager screen
- **Format:** Commonly 2 coding problems in 60 to 70 min (examples: two-bag knapsack, follow a path through a 15 x 10 grid), sometimes plus 15 to 20 MCQs on the team's stack (JavaScript/React, Java)
- **Notes:** Language may be fixed by the team (Java-only OAs reported for IS&T India). Difficulty easy to medium; time is the main constraint.

Prepare with [How to pass an OA](../online-assessments/strategy.md) and compare formats in [OA formats by company](../online-assessments/company-oa-formats.md).

## Coding rounds

- **Rounds:** 1 to 3 technical screens plus 1 to 3 onsite coding rounds (Aced); team-dependent
- **Style:** Medium LeetCode-style problems plus practical, team-mapped tasks: implement a data structure (thread-safe LRU, HashMap), Java concurrency, React components (nested tabs, rendering 1M rows), vague prompts that you must scope. Interviewers write their own questions; there is no central question bank (Aced, interviewing.io).
- **Environment:** Mostly CoderPad (copy-paste often blocked); some teams use your own laptop and IDE or a take-home assignment; FaceTime/Zoom or in person. Plan to work without AI unless told otherwise.
- **Graded on:** Working, dry-run code; data structure choice; core language depth; trade-off reasoning; communication; fit with the team's domain. Hiring manager weight is high and behavioral signal matters as much as coding for many teams (interviewing.io).
- **Reported focus topics:** Hash maps and arrays, LRU cache and other design-a-class problems (time-based key-value store, hit counter), Linked lists and heaps (merge k sorted), Graphs and topological sort (course schedule), Trees and BSTs, Intervals, Dynamic programming basics (knapsack, coin change), Concurrency and thread safety, Core language internals for the team's stack (Java, Swift, JavaScript/React), OS, networking and database fundamentals

Run every practice problem through [the 45-minute framework](../coding/interview-framework.md) and the [code quality rubric](../coding/code-quality.md).

## Beyond LeetCode

Domain deep dives in the team's stack (iOS, React output prediction and hooks, Java/Spring Boot theory, OS and networking fundamentals); take-home assignments chosen by the hiring manager (interviewing.io); presenting a past project (Aced blog); code review or design-pattern refactoring in an LLD round (Sep 2026 report); vague, scoped-by-you prompts such as a vote counter that must batch millions of ballots.

## System design

Rare for ICT2 new grads and interns, though IS&T intern candidates ask about HLD/LLD basics and India junior loops include an LLD-flavored round. ICT3 and above get team-specific design focused on reliability, privacy and on-device vs cloud trade-offs (Aced; interviewing.io notes Apple interviewers 'love to hear about reliability').

## Behavioral

**Framework:** No published interview framework; team-run behavioral and hiring manager rounds built around 'Why Apple?', collaboration, invention and results; Apple's shared values (accessibility, privacy, environment, inclusion) ([official page](https://www.apple.com/careers/us/life-at-apple/shared-values.html))

**What they look for:**

- A genuine, specific 'Why Apple?' and why this team (asked repeatedly)
- Passion for Apple products tied to your own story
- Collaboration across teams with limited guidance
- Invention: original solutions in your projects
- Results despite setbacks, with outcomes
- Depth on your own projects and technical decisions
- Discretion and comfort with a secretive, siloed culture
- How you use GenAI tools day to day (2025 to 2026 screens)

**Questions to prepare:**

- Why Apple? Why this team?
- Walk me through your resume and the work you are most proud of.
- Tell me about a time you worked in a team without sufficient guidance.
- Tell me about a time you came up with an innovative solution in a project.
- Tell me about a time you had a setback and still delivered.
- Tell me about a time you had a disagreement with a peer.
- Tell me about a time you delivered in a short time and reprioritized work.
- What GenAI tools do you use day to day, and how do they improve your efficiency?
- Given Apple values like deep expertise and drive for excellence, how have you shown them in your career?

Build your answers with the [story bank](../behavioral/story-bank.md). Company detail: [behavioral guide](../behavioral/apple.md).

## Tips

- Ask your recruiter forced-choice questions about each round (LeetCode-style vs domain deep dive, which language) because every team runs its own loop (interviewing.io).
- Prepare a specific 'Why Apple, why this team' story tied to products you use; some interviewers reject on this alone (interviewing.io).
- Study the team's stack in depth: Java concurrency and Spring for IS&T backend roles, React hooks and DOM for frontend, Swift and iOS for app teams.
- Apply to several Apple teams at once; you can run parallel processes.
- Practice medium problems on CoderPad with copy-paste off and dry-run your own tests.
- For India IS&T internships, revise CS fundamentals (networking, DNS, caching, OS, DBMS) along with DSA and your projects.
- Expect delays between rounds and after a verbal offer; follow up politely and keep other processes alive.
- Do not plan on AI help in interviews; confirm the rules with your recruiter.

## 4-week plan for Apple

Do this after you finish a core list like [Grind 75 or NeetCode 150](../coding/problem-lists.md).

- [ ] Week 1: Solve problems 1 to 20 from the most frequent list. Time-box each at 30 minutes.
- [ ] Week 2: Solve problems 21 to 40. Re-solve any you failed in week 1 without looking.
- [ ] Week 3: Solve the signature problems and every reported question above. Do 2 timed mock interviews.
- [ ] Week 4: Write 8 stories for the No published interview framework; team-run behavioral and hiring manager rounds built around 'Why Apple?', collaboration, invention and results; Apple's shared values (accessibility, privacy, environment, inclusion) round. Do 2 full mock loops. Review the online assessment and system design notes above.
