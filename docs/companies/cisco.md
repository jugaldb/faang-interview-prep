## Interview process

### New grad

1. **Application.** Official: recruiter and hiring manager review qualifications and decide who moves to interviews. Jobs are per team requisition.
2. **HackerRank online assessment.** US: emailed right after you apply (candidates say every applicant gets it); about 90 min, 2 to 4 LeetCode easy/medium problems (e.g., rotate a matrix, linked list, array counting, print a pattern); about a 2-week window. Passing all tests does not guarantee a call: resumes are reviewed after the OA.
3. **Recruiter or hiring manager screen.** Short call on background, why this role, languages you want to use. Some teams skip it.
4. **Technical interviews.** 1 to 2 rounds of 45 to 75 min, often with 2 or 3 engineers. Resume and project deep dive, LeetCode-style coding, CS fundamentals (networking, OS, C, databases), sometimes a small design (multithreading).
5. **Manager round.** Behavioral and resume questions with the hiring or engineering manager (30 to 70 min); may include an easy verbal DSA question. STAR answers expected.
6. **Offer.** Recruiter extends the offer. Responses can be slow: one SE I candidate waited about 2 months after the OA.

### Intern

1. **HackerRank OA.** Same auto-sent OA as new grads (US). India campus and off-campus intern OA (2025 to 2026): 42 questions in 90 min: about 20 MCQs and about 20 subjective CS questions (OS, networks, OOP, computer architecture, DBMS, aptitude) plus 2 long coding problems (graphs, trees, DP; struct/OOP-style solutions).
2. **Superday or screen (US).** Reported variants in 2025: (a) superday with a manager semi-behavioral round plus a technical trivia round on C code, networking and basic security; (b) a 45 min screen with two senior engineers, theory only ('what happens when you type cisco.com'); (c) a mostly networking interview ('how does ping work') followed by basic bit manipulation and building an inverted linked list (SWE II Intern, San Jose, Mar 2025); (d) three interviews with no LeetCode, focused on your stack, data processing optimization and STAR behavioral; (e) one in-person interview on networking plus LLD (design a router in Python); (f) OA then a single technical round with the hiring manager on experience, automation testing and behavioral questions.
3. **India Code with Cisco / campus.** OA, then (for Code with Cisco) a 2-day hackathon and a blind resume (no name, CGPA or college). Interview day: Technical (1 h: projects, OS/OOP scenarios, networks, a puzzle, easy DSA), Managerial (1 h: project depth), sometimes an Executive round (1 h), then ETR/HR (10 to 15 min). Spring Intern+FTE 2025 round 1 was AI-assisted coding: build 1 of 6 small services in 24 h with AI tools, document AI use, then make live changes with AI during the interview.
4. **Offer / PPO.** India interns get PPOs in tiers by project performance (one 2025 report: G4, G6, or G6 plus RSUs).

### With 1 to 3 years of experience

For 1 to 3 years (and above), a recruiter often sends a HackerRank link first (India, Mar 2026). Then: R1 technical (about 1 to 1.25 h: resume deep dive, DSA, theory such as SSH vs TLS, inner vs outer join, ACID), R2 coding plus system design or language depth (e.g., Go goroutines, channels, mutexes, WaitGroups), and an engineering manager round (project scaling, behavioral, sometimes an easy verbal DSA). One 3+ YOE candidate cleared all rounds but lost to someone with direct stack experience (Jan 2025). Cisco Meraki uses CoderPad coding rounds; Splunk (Cisco) full-stack screening used Karat (15-min MCQ then a 60-min live interview with system design discussion plus coding, with a redo option).

## Online assessment

- **Platform:** HackerRank
- **Format:** US: about 90 min, 2 to 4 LeetCode easy/medium coding problems, about a 2-week window, auto-sent after applying. India intern/new grad (campus, Code with Cisco): 42 questions in 90 min (MCQs plus subjective CS fundamentals plus 2 lengthy coding problems, often 1 hard DP and 1 medium graph).
- **Notes:** Many candidates pass every test and still get rejected, because resume screening happens after the OA. One candidate who opened the link too early got a fresh link on request (Oct 2025). Cisco's GenAI policy: using AI to prepare is fine; copying AI-generated answers into assessments or interviews is not.

Prepare with [How to pass an OA](../online-assessments/strategy.md) and compare formats in [OA formats by company](../online-assessments/company-oa-formats.md).

## Coding rounds

- **Rounds:** OA plus 1 to 2 live technical rounds (some intern loops have no live LeetCode at all).
- **Style:** LeetCode easy to medium; occasional hard in India OAs (Collect Coins in a Tree). Heavy on C fundamentals (pointers, linked lists, bit manipulation), trees/graphs/grids, and practical data parsing.
- **Environment:** HackerRank for the OA; CoderPad or a shared editor for live rounds (screen share); in-person whiteboard for some campus loops. Some 2025 India rounds used your own IDE with AI assistants.
- **Graded on:** Correctness and test cases in the OA; in interviews, depth of CS fundamentals, ability to explain and defend your own projects, communication. One CoderPad round gave zero hints (Jan 2025).
- **Reported focus topics:** Computer networks (TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP request flow, IP addressing, ping), Operating systems (process vs thread, deadlock, scheduling, memory), C and C++ (pointers, linked lists, bit manipulation, reading buggy code), Trees and graphs, BFS on grids with state, Dynamic programming (OA), OOP and low-level design, Multithreading and concurrency, DBMS (joins, ACID) and basic security (SSH, TLS), Project deep dives and behavioral (STAR)

Run every practice problem through [the 45-minute framework](../coding/interview-framework.md) and the [code quality rubric](../coding/code-quality.md).

## Beyond LeetCode

Networking trivia ('what happens when you type cisco.com', IP addressing, ping, PCIe); 'Given this C code, what problems do you see?'; C snippet and pointer questions; LLD of a router in Python; AI-assisted build-and-modify task (India 2025: Expense Splitter, IoT Sensor Monitor, Rate Limiter Monitor, Travel Itinerary Planner, Lab Resource Scheduler, Smart Home Controller); 2-day hackathon (Code with Cisco); subjective CS questions in the OA; Monte Carlo and recursion puzzles; group discussion for some India analyst roles; Karat screens for Splunk roles.

## System design

New grad and intern: light LLD or design discussion tied to your projects (design a router in Python, design multithreading, file I/O design questions, a chat/helping system; India intern loops have asked for an Instagram-like app architecture). For 3+ years: a DSA plus system design round. Prepfully's guide (last modified Jul 2024, so dated) lists HLD prompts (URL shortener, WhatsApp-style chat, ride-sharing front end) for onsite loops.

## Behavioral

**Framework:** Cisco Guiding Principles: Think really big; Play to win; Drive durable growth. STAR answers in behavioral rounds. ([official page](https://careers.cisco.com/global/en/why-work-here))

**What they look for:**

- Curiosity and eagerness to learn (official tip: 'Come curious, come hungry!')
- Authentic, specific stories about your background and accomplishments
- Deep ownership of every project on the resume (managers cross-question how you used networking or OS concepts)
- Fit and stability for the team: long-term goals aligned with the role
- Accountability and coachability ('Be real, be coachable')
- Knowledge of Cisco products and competitors (asked in India ETR)

**Questions to prepare:**

- Where do you see yourself in the next 3 to 4 years? (Winter Intern + FTE managerial round, India, Jan 2026)
- Tell me about a team conflict, someone taking credit for your work, and a time you led. (Code with Cisco, Aug 2025)
- Cisco may never fully go into AI/ML. How are you suitable then? (Code with Cisco executive round, Aug 2025)
- Why are you interested in this role? (Software Engineer I, US, Mar 2025)
- If you could be any data structure, which would you be? (Software Engineer I, US, Oct 2025)
- What programming languages do you know and want to work with? (SWE Intern, US, Feb 2025)
- Tell me about a time when things did not go as planned in a project. (Analyst campus managerial round, India, Oct 2025)

Build your answers with the [story bank](../behavioral/story-bank.md). Company detail: [behavioral guide](../behavioral/other-companies.md).

## Tips

- Take the HackerRank OA quickly but carefully; it goes to every applicant, and resume review happens after, so also tailor the resume to the team.
- Revise networking end to end: be able to walk through 'what happens when you type cisco.com'.
- Revise C: pointer bugs, linked list insert/delete, set/clear/toggle bits; one US intern technical round was 'what problems do you see in this C code'.
- Know every project line by line; Cisco managers cross-question how you used networking and OS concepts in your own work.
- Frame long-term goals around growing in the role; one India candidate felt a 'build my own startup' answer signaled misalignment.
- Follow Cisco's GenAI policy: prepare with AI, never paste AI answers. If a round is AI-assisted, use an in-IDE assistant (Copilot style); one interviewer expected that over copy-pasting from a chat window.
- India OA: 42 questions in 90 min; budget time for the long subjective CS questions and the 2 wordy coding problems.
- Learn the three Guiding Principles (Think really big, Play to win, Drive durable growth) and map a story to each.

## 4-week plan for Cisco

Do this after you finish a core list like [Grind 75 or NeetCode 150](../coding/problem-lists.md).

- [ ] Week 1: Solve problems 1 to 20 from the most frequent list. Time-box each at 30 minutes.
- [ ] Week 2: Solve problems 21 to 40. Re-solve any you failed in week 1 without looking.
- [ ] Week 3: Solve the signature problems and every reported question above. Do 2 timed mock interviews.
- [ ] Week 4: Write 8 stories for the Cisco Guiding Principles: Think really big; Play to win; Drive durable growth. STAR answers in behavioral rounds. round. Do 2 full mock loops. Review the online assessment and system design notes above.
