## Interview process

### New grad

1. **Application or recruiter outreach.** Easy Apply on LinkedIn Jobs, referral, or recruiter InMail. Official 4-stage flow: Application, Conversation (recruiter screen, possibly HM video call), Interview (multiple team members, each on a different competency), Decision (can take several weeks). Official entry-level page lists the teams that hire entry-level engineers (Developer Productivity, Frontend (UI), Site Reliability, Systems and Infrastructure, Data Science, Productivity Engineering, InfoSec); LinkedIn Engineering's official videos describe separate technical screens for the Applications, AI/ML, Front End UI, SRE, iOS and Android tracks.
2. **Recruiter call.** About 30 min: background, motivation, walkthrough of the loop. Some recruiters explain the full structure in advance.
3. **Technical phone screen (TPS).** 60 min on CoderPad, often two interviewers (primary plus shadow). 10 to 30 min of resume/project talk or CS fundamentals (Infra: process vs thread, virtual memory, DB normalization, implement a hashmap), then 1 to 2 coding problems: an easy warm-up plus a medium. Applications screens reuse LinkedIn-flavored prompts (config-file parentheses validator, KNOWN_WORDS phone keypad, seat people like Can Place Flowers, Bulb Switcher). Infra screens favor Nested List Weight Sum II with 3 to 4 follow-ups. Some interviewers say the first 'smoke test' question is not graded.
4. **Onsite loop (virtual or in-person, 4 to 6 rounds of 60 min).** Coding 1 and Coding 2: usually 2 DSA problems each, medium to hard, many from the LinkedIn tag. AI-enabled coding (added late 2025 to 2026 in some loops): CoderPad with a built-in AI assistant, a multi-part problem (LRU with counters plus cache hit/miss ratio, interval scheduling, social-graph methods). Software Design and Architecture: HLD with LLD depth. Technical Communication: present and defend a past project. Host Manager (hiring manager): behavioral, project walkthrough, a hypothetical scenario.
5. **Decision and team match.** Feedback reviewed together; borderline candidates may get an extra interview (interviewing.io). A 'select' can be parked for lack of headcount; results reported as valid for about a year, followed by a 30-min team-fit call when a team opens (Oct 2025 to Jan 2026 reports).

### Intern

1. **Online assessment (India data).** Summer 2025 Bangalore intern: 3 DSA questions in 90 min (2 medium, 1 hard, LeetCode plus competitive-programming style). Aug 2025 on-campus OA: 2 questions (binary search on answer; max subarray sum with length at most k). Aug 2026 OA for 2027/2028 grads (intern plus SWE): graph problem (cheapest round trip to any city's concert, solvable with multi-source Dijkstra). An off-campus summer-intern OA was also reported on Aug 31, 2025. Platform not named in reports.
2. **Technical interview.** 60 min, straight into 2 LeetCode mediums (both DP in the 2024 report). Explain approach, code, complexity.
3. **Host manager round.** About 45 min with an engineering manager: behavioral plus detailed walkthrough of one real project and LinkedIn tech culture. Offer came the next day in the 2024 report.
4. **US interns.** No public 2025 to 2026 US intern interview reports found. Official FAQ: applications generally open in late summer; you must return to an education program after the internship. No LinkedIn SWE intern posting surfaced in a LinkedIn Jobs search on Oct 4, 2026.

### With 1 to 3 years of experience

1 to 3 yrs: same IC2 loop as new grads (phone screen, then 2 coding, design, technical communication, host manager, sometimes an AI-enabled coding round). Track changes content: Systems and Infra adds OS/DB/concurrency questions and thread-safety follow-ups (Max Stack made multithreaded); AI/ML SWE adds ML theory, an AI coding round, and ML system design (feed second-pass ranker); Frontend adds JavaScript I/O questions, a vanilla-JS feature build, 'Pragmatic UI', design Google Sheets in vanilla JS, and a 'Product and Technical Taste' round with a PM. The HM probes on-call, ownership, why you are leaving, and asks you to run a hypothetical project end to end. Recruiters said every round must go well at this level (AI Engineer report, Dec 2025).

## Online assessment

- **Platform:** Not consistently named in reports. India campus/intern hiring uses an online test; US and experienced candidates go straight to a CoderPad phone screen.
- **Format:** India intern/new grad: 2 to 3 DSA problems in about 90 min (one hard).
- **Notes:** Recent OA topics: weighted-graph shortest paths (round-trip concert cost, solvable with multi-source Dijkstra; Aug 2026), binary search on answer and bounded-length max subarray (Aug 2025), DP. Expect competitive-programming flavor in India OAs.

Prepare with [How to pass an OA](../online-assessments/strategy.md) and compare formats in [OA formats by company](../online-assessments/company-oa-formats.md).

## Coding rounds

- **Rounds:** Phone screen (1 to 2 problems) plus 2 onsite DSA rounds (2 problems each, 60 min) plus, in some 2026 loops, 1 AI-enabled coding round.
- **Style:** LeetCode medium with frequent hards from the LinkedIn tag, many Premium-only (Nested List Weight Sum II, Find the Celebrity, Max Stack, Closest BST Value II, Find Leaves). Prompts are reworded with LinkedIn context (config validators, KNOWN_WORDS keypad) and carry 2 to 4 follow-ups.
- **Environment:** CoderPad for screens and onsites; some candidates could not run code against tests. AI-enabled round: CoderPad with an AI assistant (one candidate used Claude inside it; another reported the AI hanging for minutes). One in-person onsite asked candidates to bring a personal laptop (Nov 2025). Official policy: no AI in live interviews unless the recruiting team says AI is part of the format.
- **Graded on:** Optimal solution, correct compiling code, edge cases, dry run, and handling every follow-up. Reported reject reasons: wrong binary search bounds and C++ syntax slips on an easy screen; not reaching the precomputation follow-up on the keypad problem. AI round: quality of prompting, verifying AI output, and optimality. interviewing.io describes a 4-point scale per round with 3 as passing.
- **Reported focus topics:** Trees and BSTs (Closest BST Value II, Find Leaves, Binary Tree Upside Down, Symmetric Tree), Graphs: BFS/DFS grids and Dijkstra (Number of Islands variants, Minimum Knight Moves, Word Ladder, hidden grid), Design-a-data-structure (Max Stack, All O(1), RandomizedCollection, LRU/LFU variants), Sliding window with circular follow-ups (Max Consecutive Ones III), Math and geometry (Bulb Switcher, Max Points on a Line), Backtracking plus Trie (keypad KNOWN_WORDS), DP (Paint House, Longest Palindromic Subsequence), Heaps (Find K Pairs with Smallest Sums), Nested structures (Nested List Weight Sum I and II), System design: calendar, metrics/monitoring, top-K, search index

Run every practice problem through [the 45-minute framework](../coding/interview-framework.md) and the [code quality rubric](../coding/code-quality.md).

## Behavioral

**Framework:** LinkedIn shared values, assessed mainly in the Host Manager round and the Technical Communication round: members first; trust and care about each other; open, honest and constructive; act as One LinkedIn; diversity, inclusion and belonging. The same official page describes the culture as dreaming big, getting things done and knowing how to have fun (interviewing.io lists this as a sixth value). ([official page](https://careers.linkedin.com/culture-and-values))

**What they look for:**

- End-to-end ownership of a real project you can defend for 60 minutes
- Trade-off reasoning and what you would change in hindsight
- Clear explanation to a non-expert (explain your project to a new intern)
- On-call and incident handling
- Cross-team collaboration and how you approach ambiguity (which teams you would involve, POCs)
- Product sense (Frontend 'Product and Technical Taste' round)

**Questions to prepare:**

- Why LinkedIn? Why are you leaving your current company?
- What does on-call look like in your current team?
- Give two positives and two negatives about your manager.
- How do you handle context switching and resolve conflicts?
- How do you handle pressure and difficult situations?
- Explain one of your projects to a new intern.
- Here is an ambiguous problem: which teams would you reach out to and how would you structure POCs?
- Tell me about a project you are proud of and its hardest part.

Build your answers with the [story bank](../behavioral/story-bank.md). Company detail: [behavioral guide](../behavioral/other-companies.md).

## Tips

- Work through the LinkedIn company tag on LeetCode; many repeat problems are Premium-only (Nested List Weight Sum II, Find the Celebrity, Max Stack, Closest BST Value II, Find Leaves), so one month of Premium before the loop pays off.
- Make easy screen questions flawless: 2025 to 2026 rejects came from off-by-one binary search, syntax slips, and missing the precomputation follow-up on the keypad problem.
- Practice the KNOWN_WORDS keypad problem with a Trie plus add/remove follow-ups; it recurs across Applications screens from mid-2025 to 2026.
- Pick your track on purpose: Systems and Infra screens add OS, DB and concurrency fundamentals; Applications screens lean on LinkedIn-flavored easy/medium prompts.
- Prepare one project for the 60-min Technical Communication round: architecture diagram, each decision and its trade-off, what you would change, and an explanation simple enough for a new intern.
- Rehearse AI-assisted coding: steer the assistant, test and verify its output, and push for the optimal solution. Use AI only when the recruiter says the round allows it.
- Study system design even at IC2: Google Calendar and metrics/monitoring are the most repeated prompts, and a weak design round alone can sink the loop.
- Ask the recruiter which rounds you have (AI-enabled coding or not, Applications vs Infra) and whether the first screen question is graded.
